What to Plant & Protect Right Now in November (South Coastal BC)

2026-11-20 • Seasonal Planting Guide

November in Surrey & Zone 8a is prime window for planting dormant native shrubs and oaks before winter freezes, direct-sowing cold-stratified meadow seeds (Camas, Nodding Onion, Lupine), and mulching garlic & overwintered brassicas.

South Coastal BC's mild maritime November rain creates ideal soil moisture for establishing native woody perennials without summer irrigation stress.

1. Native Shrubs & Trees to Plant Now: Bare-root and 1-gallon Nootka Rose (Rosa nutkana), Red-Flowering Currant (Ribes sanguineum), Saskatoon Serviceberry (Amelanchier alnifolia), Salal (Gaultheria shallon), Western Sword Fern (Polystichum munitum), and Garry Oak (Quercus garryana) establish deep winter roots right now.

2. Fall Seeds Needing Natural Cold Stratification: Sow Great Camas (Camassia leichtlinii), Common Camas, Nodding Onion (Allium cernuum), Broad-Leaved Shooting Star, and Large-Leaved Lupine outdoors before December so natural winter chill breaks seed dormancy.

3. Vegetable Beds & Frost Care: Finish planting Hardneck Seed Garlic cloves before sustained frost, cover Lettuce, Swiss Chard, and Lacinato Kale with breathable row cover when temperatures dip below 4°C, and top-dress Asparagus, Rhubarb, and Strawberry crowns with leaf mulch.

December Coastal Gardening: Dormant Live-Staking, Cold Frames & Early Seed Orders

2026-11-20 • Seasonal Planting Guide

Keep cool-season kale and leeks ventilated in cold frames, take dormant hardwood cuttings of Red-Osier Dogwood and Pacific Ninebark, and reserve spring vegetable & native plant pre-orders.

Even as frost advisories arrive in Surrey and the Lower Mainland, December offers rewarding stewardship tasks for resilient spring gardens.

1. Dormant Hardwood Live-Staking: Take pencil-thick dormant cuttings of Red-Osier Dogwood (Cornus sericea), Pacific Ninebark (Physocarpus capitatus), and Salmonberry (Rubus spectabilis) and push them directly into moist riparian or rain-garden soil.

2. Cold-Frame & Cloche Management: Prop open cold frames on sunny afternoons to prevent fungal damping-off on overwintering Lettuce, Spinach, and Corn Salad (Mâche).

3. Planning January Starts & Pre-Orders: Audit seed packets and reserve spring starts early—such as Sungold Cherry Tomatoes, Sweet Bell Peppers, and potted Garry Oaks—via our Live Plant Orders & Pre-Orders page.

Designing High-Pollinator Coastal Spring Guilds: Native Shrubs, Camas & Companion Beds

2026-11-18 • Pollinators & Ecology

Combine early-blooming Red-Flowering Currant and Oregon Grape with Camas, Yarrow, Basil, and Marigolds to sustain bumblebees, mason bees, and Rufous Hummingbirds from March through autumn.

Early spring pollinators in Coastal BC emerge as soon as daytime temperatures reach 10–12°C, often before orchard or vegetable crops flower.

1. Early Spring Nectar Anchors: Ribes sanguineum (Red-Flowering Currant) and Mahonia aquifolium (Tall Oregon Grape) feed queen Bombus bumblebees, Osmia mason bees, and migrating Rufous Hummingbirds in March–April.

2. Summer Meadow & Companion Guilds: Layer Achillea millefolium (Common Yarrow), Allium cernuum (Nodding Onion), and Lupinus polyphyllus alongside Tomato, Sweet Pepper, and Genovese Sweet Basil beds to attract predatory hoverflies, lacewings, and parasitoid wasps.

Summer Drought Resilience in Zone 8a: Deep-Rooted Natives & Living Mulches

2026-11-15 • Soil & Waterwise Care

Reduce summer watering in Metro Vancouver & Fraser Valley gardens using drought-adapted Garry Oak, Woolly Sunflower, Common Yarrow, and Coastal Wild Strawberry groundcovers.

South Coastal BC summers regularly bring 6–8 weeks of dry weather in July and August. Pairing deep-rooted native perennials with dense living groundcovers keeps soil temperatures cool and reduces evaporation.

• Sunny Dry Slopes: Pair Eriophyllum lanatum (Woolly Sunflower), Achillea millefolium (Common Yarrow), and Arctostaphylos uva-ursi (Kinnikinnick).
• Shaded Understory: Polystichum munitum (Western Sword Fern) and Gaultheria shallon (Salal) stabilize slopes under conifers while staying evergreen all year.
