Frequently Asked Questions (Drought-Tolerant in Coastal BC)

Do drought-tolerant native plants still need water in their first year?

Yes — give new plantings deep, infrequent soakings once a week during dry stretches in their first 1–2 summers while roots dive deep. After year two, most need little or no supplemental water.

What are the best BC native plants for hot, sunny boulevard strips (hellstrips)?

Kinnikinnick (Arctostaphylos uva-ursi), Broad-leaved Stonecrop (Sedum spathulifolium), Western Yarrow, Pearly Everlasting, Nodding Onion, and Roemer's Fescue thrive in lean, baking soil.

Can I replace a thirsty lawn with drought-tolerant BC natives?

Yes — low-growing groundcovers like Kinnikinnick, Coastal Strawberry, Wild Thyme-leaved natives, and dry meadow grasses/bulbs (Camas, Woolly Sunflower) cut summer water use dramatically.

What kind of mulch helps native plants survive Fraser Valley summer heat?

A 5–8 cm layer of coarse arborist wood chips around shrubs (kept off the crown) cools roots and holds spring moisture well into August; rock-garden succulents prefer coarse gravel mulch instead.
