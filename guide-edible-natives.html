Growing edible native shrubs and perennials in your own yard reconnects local food gardening with wildlife habitat and regional ecology.

Frequently Asked Questions (Edible & Traditional in Coastal BC)

What are the easiest edible BC native berry shrubs to grow in a backyard?

Evergreen Huckleberry, Salal, Thimbleberry, Salmonberry, Saskatoon (Serviceberry), Trailing Blackberry, and Nootka Rose (rosehips) are productive, low-maintenance, and well-suited to Surrey and Metro Vancouver gardens.

Are all edible-listed native plants safe to eat raw?

No — several native plants require specific preparation, cooking, or harvesting only certain parts (for example, Red Elderberry berries must be cooked and seeds/stems avoided, and Death Camas closely resembles Edible Camas when not in bloom). Always confirm 100% botanical ID and traditional preparation before consuming any plant.

Where do the Coast Salish plant names come from?

Coast Salish and First Nations plant names and ethnobotanical notes represent Traditional Knowledge (TK) belonging to their respective Nations (including hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓, Sḵwx̱wú7mesh, and SENĆOŦEN communities) and are shared here for respectful education.

Can I grow an edible native food forest in a small urban Metro Vancouver lot?

Yes — stack a small tree like Pacific Crabapple or Saskatoon with shade-tolerant Evergreen Huckleberry and Salal underneath, and edge sunny paths with Coastal Strawberry and Nodding Onion.
