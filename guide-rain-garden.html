Coastal BC Rain Garden & Wet Soil Native Plants

Metro Vancouver and the Fraser Valley receive over 1,200–1,800 mm of rain each year, leaving clay yards and downspout zones waterlogged from October to April. Native wetland and riparian plants turn soggy trouble spots into natural stormwater filters that recharge groundwater and support amphibians and pollinators.

Frequently Asked Questions (Rain Gardens in Coastal BC)

How do you zone plants inside a Coastal BC rain garden?

Place water-loving sedges, rushes, Skunk Cabbage, and Pacific Ninebark in the deepest wet basin (Zone 1); Red-Osier Dogwood, Twinberry, and Goat's Beard on the moist slope (Zone 2); and drier-tolerant natives on the outer rim (Zone 3).

What native plants survive both winter flooding and dry BC summers?

Slough Sedge, Common Rush, Red-Osier Dogwood, Pacific Ninebark, Douglas Spirea, and Nootka Rose handle standing winter water and adapt smoothly once summer rain stops.

How far should a rain garden be from a house foundation in Surrey or Vancouver?

Most Metro Vancouver municipal stormwater guidelines recommend placing rain garden basins at least 3 metres (10 feet) downslope from building foundations and away from septic fields.

Do rain gardens breed mosquitoes?

A properly excavated rain garden drains standing water within 24–48 hours — far shorter than the 7–10 days mosquito larvae need to develop.
