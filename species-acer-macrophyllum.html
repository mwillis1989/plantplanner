Bigleaf Maple

Acer macrophyllum Pursh · Sapindaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowTreeZone 4a–9b🦋 20 Lepidoptera hosts
🔎 Open 5-Tab Explorer Profile📊 Compare in Native Plant MatrixE-Flora BC ↗

Overview

Summary

Native, long-lived medium to large deciduous tree to 30 m tall; simple opposite leaves 10-30 cm wide; yellow fragrant flowers in nodding racemes; paired brown winged seeds with stiff hair. Thriving in forests, riparian areas, and woodlands, as well as moist to mesic forests and open slopes in the lowland and montane zones, this species is common in southwestern British Columbia west of the Coast-Cascade Mountains and extends south to California, with disjunct populations in ID.

Botanical Description

This majestic deciduous tree can reach a towering height of 30 meters, its greenish bark smoothing with age and eventually giving way to a greyish-brown, textured surface often adorned with lichens and mosses. Its large, deeply lobed leaves, spanning 10-30 cm in width, create a lush canopy that transitions to a vibrant yellow in autumn. The tree produces an abundance of greenish-white flowers, followed by distinctive yellowish-brown, hairy winged fruits arranged in a V-shape. This magnificent tree is a commanding presence in any landscape, offering beauty and grandeur throughout the seasons.

Name Etymology

Acer means sharp in Latin, referring to pointed leaves, and macrophyllum means large-leaved.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Thriving in forests, riparian areas, and woodlands, as well as moist to mesic forests and open slopes in the lowland and montane zones, this species is common in southwestern British Columbia west of the Coast-Cascade Mountains and extends south to California, with disjunct populations in ID. The Coast region spans from southeastern Alaska through the Cascades and Sierra Nevada to California, inhabiting coarse, gravelly dry or moist sites, foothills, streams, and river bottoms. Acer macrophyllum is distributed around the coast region of southeastern Alaska, on the west side of the Cascades and Sierra Nevada from British Columbia through most of California.

Habitat Types

Forests; Woodlands; Riparian & Streambanks; Wetlands & Marshes; Marine Shorelines

Vancouver Island; Gulf Islands; Lower Mainland; Coast Mountains; Southern Interior; West of Cascade Crest; East of Cascade Crest

Elevation

Lowland to Montane (0–1200 m)

Successional Role

Deciduous, Shade-tolerant, long-lived. Wind protection is very good.

Fire Ecology

Establishes after fire of light to medium intensity and in openings created by dying trees. Pseudotsuga "Fire Climax" succession.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Woodland / Shade; Rock Garden; Rain Garden / Wetland; Coastal / Bluff

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Drought Tolerant; Erosion Control; Edible

Soil Preferences

Acer macrophyllum generally occurs in coarse, gravelly, dry to moist sites, often mixed with red alder, western redcedar, Douglas fir, and western hemlock. It attains its best development near borders of foothills, low mountain streams, and in alluvial river bottoms.

Moisture Notes

Adapted to well-drained winter-wet soils with prolonged summer dry periods.

Spread Behaviour

Woody crown and lateral root system

Root Formation

Deep lateral / Taproot / Woody burl

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota endomycorrhizal network)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic and non-aggressive (Deep lateral / Taproot / Woody burl); low root competition makes it safe for intimate mixed borders and diverse polyculture interplanting.

Erosion & Slope Control

Plant on laminated rot sites; accelerates nutrient cycling, site productivity, revegetates disturbed riparian areas; Slope & bank stabilization.

Pruning & Management

Pruning should be done in the winter or early spring to remove the weakest branches to allow for the passage of more light.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Edible blossoms and sprouts. Sap can be collected for syrup. Dried leaves used as flavoring.

Edible Parts & Flavour

Flowers; Inner bark ground into powder for soup thickener/bread.

Material & Technology

A fiber was obtained from the inner bark and used in making ropes, baskets, and crude dresses. Tough, hard wood carved into canoe paddles ("paddle tree"), spindle whorls, bowls, dishes, masks, and tools. Inner bark used for baskets, rope, and whisks.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

An infusion of the bark was used in the treatment of tuberculosis. A sticky gum obtained from the buds in the spring was mixed with oil and used as a hair tonic. Tree preparations used for sore throats and rubbed on young men's faces to stop thick whisker growth (W̱SÁNEĆ (Saanich)).

Safety

Edibility

Yes

Pets, Children & Livestock

Generally non-toxic; harvest away from roadsides and pesticides, and confirm species ID before consumption.

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Recorded insect/host associations (beees sheet): Neoalcis californiaria: on leaves; Proteoteras aesculana: on leaves

Propagation & Seeds

Stratification & Dormancy

Cold stratification required.

Sowing Protocol

Maple keys may be one of the most identifiable fruits in our region with each bearing two seeds and the winged fruits designed to travel through the air like a helicopter. The large seeds don't need to be extracted ahead of sowing, Instead, you can snap the "wing" off to plant the seed still wrapped in the protective coating. At our nursery, we sow them soon after they fall to the ground through fall, but many stay attached directly to the trees into winter.

Propagation Methods

Propagated by sowing fresh seed in fall or cold stratifying stored seed 2–4 months.
