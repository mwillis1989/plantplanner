Overview

Summary

Aromatic rhizomatous perennial herb to 30-100 cm tall; dense rosette of fern-like leaves; terminal flat-topped clusters of 5 white to pink ray flowers and cream/yellow disk flowers. Moderately rhizomatous, long-lived perennial forb, 30-100 cm tall; unbranched erect lanate stems; alternate pinnately dissected semi-evergreen aromatic leaves; flat compound corymb flower cluster; 3-12 white to cream ray flowers, 10-75 yellow disk flowers; flattened smooth and hairless dry single-seeded fruit.

Botanical Description

This aromatic perennial herb bears fern-like leaves and a profusion of compact flower heads in a flat-topped cluster. The flowers, ranging from white to pink or reddish, surround a center of creamy disk flowers. Adaptable to various environments, this versatile plant thrives in coastal regions, montane zones, and even alpine areas. Its unique varieties offer diverse foliage and flower characteristics.

Name Etymology

Achillea honors Achilles, who used the plant for wounds, and millefolium means thousand leaves.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Found in meadows, rocky slopes, and beaches, as well as mesic to dry coastal bluffs, grasslands, and open forests across all vegetation zones, this species is common throughout British Columbia, ranging north to Alaska, Yukon Territory, and Northwest Territories, east to Newfoundland, and south to Florida, Texas, California, and Mexico. It occupies dry to moist open sites at low to high elevations, disturbed poor soils, roadsides, hills, canyons, pastures, sagebrush areas, and beaches, spanning natural and disturbed habitats throughout the western United States and exhibiting a circumboreal distribution from Alaska to Mexico. It thrives in full sun on roadsides, hills, canyons, pastures, sagebrush, open timber, and subalpine zones, preferring droughty gravelly loams and shallow silty steep sites. Common yarrow can be found in pastures, grasslands, woodlands, waste grounds, open forests, prairies, fallow fields, pathway edges, subalpine zones, and exposed ridges (Chandler et al., 1982).

Erosion & Slope Control

Conservation Practices: Common yarrow has been used in the United States for land restoration of disturbed sites, installation of low maintenance sites, and erosion control of steep slopes (Johnson & Whitwell, 1997). Fast-germinating, low-growing rhizomatous cover; controls erosion due to basal leaves and extensive rhizomes; quick ground cover on degraded soil, roadsides, mined lands; drought and acid soil tolerant; holds soil on slopes.

Pruning & Management

Common yarrow can be slow to grow in diverse environments and prefers well drained soils. It is fairly drought tolerant and requires little to no irrigation.

Fall & Winter Interest

Year-round evergreen foliage anchors winter garden structure.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

The Cherokee drank a tea. Food (tea from leaves). Seed heads in tea calm nerves/muscles.

Edible Parts & Flavour

Leaves (Tea); mosquito repellant; seed heads in tea calm nerves/muscles; Tea for stomach ailments; teas, oils, salves for healing.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

The Pawnee used the stalk in a treatment for pain relief. The Chippewa used the leaves in a steam inhalant for headaches. Historically, common yarrow has been used by Native American tribes and Greeks for its medicinal properties since the Trojan War. Blood purifier, for colds and coughs, stomach/digestive ailments, and during labour. Prepared as tonic and blood purifier.

Pets, Children & Livestock

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Common yarrow does not usually appear in wetlands but can occasionally be found in riparian habitats. Common yarrow may suffer from mildew or root rot if not planted in well-drained soil. Common yarrow is susceptible to gray mold, root rot in poorly drained soils, and powdery mildew. Pests include common leaf bugs, flea beetles, and spittlebugs found mostly in the Great Plains region and the Midwest (Schuster, n.d; Warwick & Black, 1982); Common yarrow can be considered weedy or invasive in some regions.

Propagation & Seeds

Sowing Protocol

Common yarrow is a drought tolerant species of which there are several different ornamental cultivars. Plant the seeds no more than ¼ inch deep due to the need of light for germination. Prepare soil by tilling or loosening the top 30.5–38.1 cm of soil and plant from September to April indoors or outdoors (USDA-NRCS, 2014).

Propagation Methods

There are many ornamental cultivars of common yarrow with a variety of flower colors such as white, cream, pink, yellow, red, and more (Schalau, 2009).
