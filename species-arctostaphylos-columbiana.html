Hairy Manzanita

Arctostaphylos columbiana Piper. · Ericaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowShrubZone 4a–8b🦋 18 Lepidoptera hosts
🔎 Open 5-Tab Explorer Profile📊 Compare in Native Plant MatrixE-Flora BC ↗

Overview

Summary

Mediterranean-climate shrub similar to arbutus; pink flowers (6-7 mm), fruit persisting in August. Inhabits rocky outcrops and rocky slopes, as well as dry open forests in the lowland and montane zones; infrequent in extreme southwestern British Columbia, extending south along the coast to California.

Botanical Description

An erect or spreading Mediterranean-climate shrub 1-3 m tall with old branches possessing purplish-red bark and young twigs and stalks that are greyish-hairy and typically distinctly bristly with longer, scattered, glandular hairs. Leaves are alternate, evergreen, egg- or lance-shaped to elliptic, 2-5 cm long, 1-2.5 cm wide, narrowed at the tip, finely greyish-hairy particularly beneath, on stalks about 5 mm long. Belonging to the Heath Family (Ericaceae), hairy manzanita functions as an erect, much-branched evergreen shrub or small tree 1-3 m high with a broad, rounded, dense canopy, lacking a basal burl and thus unable to crown sprout after fire. Young twig bark is densely white-hirsute, while mature bark is smooth and dark red-brown.

Name Etymology

Genus Arctostaphylos; geographic specific epithet columbiana denotes the plant's North American regional distribution.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Inhabits rocky outcrops and rocky slopes, as well as dry open forests in the lowland and montane zones; infrequent in extreme southwestern British Columbia, extending south along the coast to California. Hairy manzanita inhabits well-drained, rocky slopes below 762 m from southern British Columbia south to northwestern California.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Soil Preferences

Its general geographic range is characterized by cool, relatively dry summers and wet winters with precipitation ranging from 127–304.8 cm per year. Hairy manzanita prefers well-drained, acidic soils in open sunny sites.

Moisture Notes

Adapted to well-drained winter-wet soils with prolonged summer dry periods.

Spread Behaviour

Deep spreading root system with woody basal burl capable of post-fire resprouting

Root Formation

Woody Burl & Spreading Roots

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbutoid Mycorrhiza (Ectomycorrhizal & Ericoid forest fungal network)

Competition / Allelopathy

Forms tight ericaceous root mats and phenolic leaf litter that gradually acidifies surface humus and slows annual weed invasion; compatible with acid-tolerant woodland companions.

Erosion & Slope Control

Land reclamation.

Pruning & Management

Under natural conditions, no special management is required to maintain established manzanitas. Either scarified seeds or well-rooted container plants may be used to re-vegetate cleared sites. In the urban landscape, several horticultural techniques should be used to ensure healthy plants. All manzanitas should be planted higher than the surrounding soil to prevent crown rot, which can result from excessive water and soil moisture, especially during the summer.

Fall & Winter Interest

Year-round evergreen foliage anchors winter garden structure.
