Red Three-awn

Aristida purpurea Nutt. · Poaceae

Overview

Summary

Occurs in dry grasslands, shrublands, and forest openings in the steppe and lower montane zones; frequent in south-central British Columbia, becoming less frequent eastward in southern British Columbia; extending east to southwestern Manitoba and south to Louisiana, Texas, New Mexico, Arizona, and California. Purple threeawn is commonly found on dry plains with gravelly or sandy soils, though occasionally on clay soils, and often associates with sand dropseed and grama species across its native range in western North America from Canada into Mexico.

Botanical Description

A perennial, strongly tufted grass from fibrous roots with hollow, minutely rough stems mostly (15) 20-40 cm tall (ranging from 24.1–90.2 cm / 25 to 90 cm in length); open sheaths; mostly basal blades 1-2 mm wide, 7-25 cm long (2.5–24.1 cm / 3.0 to 25 cm long, 1–2 mm / 0.07-0.2 cm wide), typically involute with flat bases, and minutely rough; and ligules scarcely 0.5 mm long, membranous, with frontal margins bearing hairs 2-3 mm long. Purple threeawn is a slender, densely cespitose perennial bunchgrass featuring a sparingly branched panicle inflorescence. Glumes are unequal with the lower glume shorter than the upper, colored light to dark brown or purple. Awns range from 6.1–14 cm (6.0 to 14 cm) in length, with the central awn thicker than the lateral awns.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Occurs in dry grasslands, shrublands, and forest openings in the steppe and lower montane zones; frequent in south-central British Columbia, becoming less frequent eastward in southern British Columbia; extending east to southwestern Manitoba and south to Louisiana, Texas, New Mexico, Arizona, and California. Purple threeawn is commonly found on dry plains with gravelly or sandy soils, though occasionally on clay soils, and often associates with sand dropseed and grama species across its native range in western North America from Canada into Mexico. However, it occasionally occurs on clay soils as well (Hatch and Pluhar 1993). This colonizer species is often associated with sand dropseed [Sporobolus virginicus (L.) Kunth] and grama (Boutelous spp.) species.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Key Features

Erosion Control

Soil Preferences

Purple threeawn is adapted to dry, recently disturbed sites.

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Spread Behaviour

Tufted non-spreading clump

Root Formation

Fibrous

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota endomycorrhizal network)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic and non-aggressive (Fibrous); low root competition makes it safe for intimate mixed borders and diverse polyculture interplanting.

Pruning & Management

Avoid grazing of new plantings for 90 days to allow plants to become established. Purple threeawn readily reseeds itself with minimal soil disturbance. Allow plants to produce seed annually to maintain stand health.

Safety

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

There are no potential problems or pests associated with purple threeawn. There are no environmental concerns associated with purple threeawn.

Propagation & Seeds

Seed Collection

Seed harvest is possible using a variety of methods and implements.

Sowing Protocol

Begin seedbed preparation well in advance of planting. Establish a clean, weed-free seedbed by either tillage or herbicides. Prior to planting, the site should be firm and have accumulated soil moisture.

Propagation Methods

Menard Germplasm purple threeawn was cooperatively released in 2020 by the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS), James E. “Bud” Smith Plant Materials Center, Knox City, Texas. Purple threeawn is best started using greenhouse grown transplants and planted into bedded rows.
