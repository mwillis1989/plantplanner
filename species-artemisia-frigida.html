Prairie Sagewort

Artemisia frigida Willd. · Asteraceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowWildflower (Forb)Zone 1a–7b🦋 2 Lepidoptera hosts

Overview

Summary

Found on dry southerly slopes, sandy banks, grasslands, and open forests in the montane to subalpine zones, this circumboreal species is common throughout British Columbia in and east of the Coast-Cascade Mountains, ranging north to Alaska, the Yukon Territory, and the Northwest Territories, east to Manitoba, and south to Minnesota, Wisconsin, Kansas, Texas, New Mexico, and Arizona, with representation in Eurasia.

Botanical Description

Prairie sagewort, belonging to the Sunflower Family (Asteraceae), is a pleasantly fragrant, spreading perennial shrublet 10-40 cm tall (1-4 dm / 9.9–39.9 cm tall) arising from a stout stem-base or tough, woody crown, often shrubby at the base with several short, leafy offsets. Stems are ascending to erect, white or tawny, and woolly-hairy (whitish or grayish tomentose, having dense, velvety, fuzzy hairs). Basal leaves are few and soon deciduous. Stem leaves are silvery-silky hairy, tomentose, abundant, clustered toward the base and scattered along the stem; lower leaves are petiolate (short-stalked below, 12 mm / 13 mm long), becoming sessile or short-stalked above.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Material & Technology

Leaves used for dye.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

The Blackfeet chewed the leaves of prairie sagewort for heartburn, and applied the leaves to wounds to reduce swelling. Medicine, fragrant grass. The roots and tops were boiled and drank as a tea for “mountain fever.” Other tribes, who used this species of sage include the Arapaho, Comanche, Gros Ventre, Cree, Navaho, Tewa, and Ute (Nickerson 1966, Carlson and, Denig 1855, Elmore 1944), Robbins et al. 1916, Cheyenne women used prairie sagewort to correct menstrual irregularity. Root or leaf tea taken by women during menstrual periods.

Pets, Children & Livestock

Generally non-toxic; harvest away from roadsides and pesticides, and confirm species ID before consumption.

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Contains volatile oils classified by the FDA as hazardous in concentrated internal doses.

Propagation & Seeds

Stratification & Dormancy

Germination is improved with a cold treatment and seed should be broadcast or planted shallowly ( 3 mm deep).

Propagation Methods

Consult your local nurseries to choose the right cultivar for your specific landscape. Artemisia species can be propagated by seeds, by division of the rootstock, or by cuttings taken in the early summer.
