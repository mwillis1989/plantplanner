Overview

Summary

A biennial or short-lived perennial native mustard serving as an important host plant for Julia's Orangetip and Sara's Orangetip butterflies, growing in sandy or rocky sites in full sun with winter moisture. Occurring on rocky slopes, meadows, and shorelines, as well as moist streambanks, meadows, and forests across lowland, steppe, and montane zones; common along the coast and less frequent elsewhere in British Columbia; ranging north to Alaska, Yukon Territory, and Northwest Territories, east to Newfoundland, and south to Minnesota, New Hampshire, Colorado, Arizona, and California.

Botanical Description

A biennial or short-lived perennial native mustard serving as an important host plant for Julia's Orangetip and Sara's Orangetip butterflies, growing in sandy or rocky sites in full sun with winter moisture. Grows in sandy or rocky sites in full sun, especially with winter moisture. Biennial (occasionally perennial) from a taproot and a simple stem-base; stems stiff, erect, angled, branched, (10) 20-60 cm tall. Basal leaves are pinnately lobed, long-stalked, less than 12 cm long, with 1-5 pairs of lateral lobes and a largest terminal lobe that is egg-shaped and entire or irregularly toothed, with stalks and sometimes leaflets long-fringed or sparsely short-hairy.
