Tall Oregon-grape

Berberis aquifolium · Berberidaceae

Overview

Summary

Rhizomatous Evergreen Shrub 1 to 3 m height with tough glossy spiny holly-like leaves, creeping rhizomes, bright yellow flower clusters, and blue/purple edible berries. Found in meadows, woodlands, thickets, dry slopes, mixed coniferous woods, and the understory of Littlewood Forest, as well as mesic to dry open or closed forests in lowland, steppe, and montane zones; common in southern British Columbia; extending east to Alberta and south to Idaho and Oregon.

Botanical Description

Evergreen perennial shrub with glossy, prominently toothed leaves that have variable colouration depending on the season (red, yellow, green). Clusters of bright yellow flowers attract bees in early spring, turning to large light blue berries in summer for birds. This plant is sun-loving, but also grows well in partial shade sites. An evergreen perennial shrub 1 to 3 m in height with creeping rhizomes, yellowish bark and wood, and branched, stoloniferous to stiffly erect stems 0.5-2.5 (4.5) m tall.

Name Etymology

Berberis comes from Arabic for barberry, and aquifolium means sharp-leaved, like holly.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Roots eaten peeled. Food (fruits intended for jam). Berries eaten raw or dried into cakes for storage/trade, often mixed with salal to dilute sourness.

Edible Parts & Flavour

Fruits (jam intended); Edible sour fruits intended for jam or eaten raw/dried in cakes; root/leaf teas as digestive tonics and decongestants.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Root tinctures and leaf/stem teas used as bitter tonics to stimulate digestion, as anti-bacterial, and decongestant. Berries used as a mild laxative. Root/leaf teas as digestive tonics and decongestants.
