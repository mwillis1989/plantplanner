Overview

Summary

Evergreen Shrub, 0.25 - 0.8 m height with purple-green/long slender veined holly-like foliage, yellow blossoms, and blue/purple fruits with white waxy coating. Inhabiting forests, woodlands, and shady coniferous woods in partial sun or shade on dry to moist soils; occurring in mesic to dry open slopes and open forests across lowland, steppe, and montane zones; common in southwestern British Columbia and infrequent eastward in southern British Columbia; ranging south to California.

Botanical Description

Evergreen Shrub, 0.25 - 0.8 m height with purple-green/long slender veined holly-like foliage, yellow blossoms, and blue/purple fruits with white waxy coating. An evergreen shrub 0.25-0.8 m in height with yellowish bark and wood, featuring branched, erect, leafy stems 10-60 cm tall. Leaves are evergreen, hollylike, turning reddish in the fall, alternate, and pinnate, comprising 9-19 palmately nerved leaflets with spiny teeth. Bears yellow blossoms and blue/purple fruits with a white waxy coating.

Name Etymology

Berberis comes from Arabic for barberry, and nervosa means veined, describing its prominently veined leaves.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Roots eaten peeled. Food (fruits intended for jam). Tart blue berries used for jellies and food.

Edible Parts & Flavour

Fruits (jam intended); Fruits (great for jam).

Material & Technology

Inner bark yields yellow dye. Roots yield yellow dye.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Prepared as a cholagogue to increase bile flow and treat digestive tract issues, infections, skin conditions, tuberculosis, hepatitis, kidney disease, and urinary tract disorders. Cholagogue action used by Native Americans for years to increase bile flow for digestive issues. Natural antibiotic and antibacterial properties. Root and bark decoctions used for blood purifier and digestive tonic.
