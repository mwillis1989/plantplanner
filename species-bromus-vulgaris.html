Overview

Summary

Perennial grass characterized by soft broad foliage and a nodding flower cluster of an open panicle featuring large, drooping, yellow spikelet flowers; standing 60-100 cm in height; arising from fibrous roots and sometimes rooting at the lower nodes; stems usually hairy at the nodes, reaching 60-100 (120) cm tall; sheaths strongly soft-hairy to smooth, frequently bearing a few long hairs at the throats; leaf blades 5-10 (15) mm wide, flat, lax, smooth to soft-hairy on either or both surfaces; leaf-bases lacking ear-shaped lobes; ligules smooth to hairy, finely to coarsely jagged, measuring (2) 3-5 mm long. Inhabiting forests and woodlands, as well as moist to mesic meadows, thickets, streambanks, rocky slopes, and open forests in the lowland and subalpine zones; common on southern Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands, infrequent in southern and central BC; ranging east to AB and south to WY, UT, ID, and CA.

Botanical Description

Perennial grass characterized by soft broad foliage and a nodding inflorescence of an open panicle featuring large, drooping, yellow spikelet flowers; standing 60-100 cm in height; arising from fibrous roots and sometimes rooting at the lower nodes; stems usually hairy at the nodes, reaching 60-100 (120) cm tall; sheaths strongly soft-hairy to smooth, frequently bearing a few long hairs at the throats; leaf blades 5-10 (15) mm wide, flat, lax, smooth to soft-hairy on either or both surfaces; leaf-bases lacking ear-shaped lobes; ligules smooth to hairy, finely to coarsely jagged, measuring (2) 3-5 mm long.

Name Etymology

Genus Bromus comes from Ancient Greek bromos ('oat'); specific epithet vulgaris means common.
