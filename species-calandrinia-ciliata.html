Red Maids

Overview

Summary

Taprooted Annual Forb, 5-15 cm height with bright hot-pink flowers. Meadows, bluffs, and rocky slopes.

Botanical Description

Distinctive small annual with outstandingly bright hot-pink flowers. Often found in maritime meadows. Very drought-tolerant, appreciates drainage. Flowers close at night and in inclement weather.

Name Etymology

Genus Calandrinia; species Calandrinia ciliata.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Meadows, bluffs, and rocky slopes. Fringed redmaids thrive in open grasslands as well as disturbed areas and cultivated fields below 2200m. The plants prefer full sun and moderately dry to moist conditions.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Redmaid seeds and greens were an important food source for many tribes in California including those whose territory neighbored the Salinan. The shiny black seeds were a major ingredient in pinole (from the Aztec word for seed flour, pinolli) and were valued for their high oil content. Small black oil-rich seeds gathered, parched, and ground into nutritious meal.
