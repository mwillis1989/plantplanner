Red Maids

Calandrinia ciliata (Ruiz & Pavon) DC. · Portulacaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowWildflower (Forb)Zone 4a–9b

Overview

Summary

Taprooted Annual Forb, 5-15 cm height with bright hot-pink flowers. Meadows, bluffs, and rocky slopes.

Botanical Description

Distinctive small annual with outstandingly bright hot-pink flowers. Often found in maritime meadows. Very drought-tolerant, appreciates drainage. Flowers close at night and in inclement weather.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Meadows, bluffs, and rocky slopes. Fringed redmaids thrive in open grasslands as well as disturbed areas and cultivated fields below 2200m. The plants prefer full sun and moderately dry to moist conditions.

Habitat Types

Meadows; Prairies; Rock Outcrops & Bluffs; Thickets & Clearings

Fire Ecology

Calandrinia ciliata is an early colonizer of disturbed areas. It increases in abundance following a fire because of the increase in nutrients and the decrease in competing grasses. The native Californians burned grasslands periodically to increase harvests of redmaids as well as other fire-following species.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Redmaid seeds and greens were an important food source for many tribes in California including those whose territory neighbored the Salinan. The shiny black seeds were a major ingredient in pinole (from the Aztec word for seed flour, pinolli) and were valued for their high oil content. Small black oil-rich seeds gathered, parched, and ground into nutritious meal.

Material & Technology

The seeds were separated by shaking or hitting the plants over the cleaned area or into a basket. Then they were placed into a tightly woven winnowing basket where they were tossed, allowing the moving air to remove any chaff. The seeds were then dried for storage or parched in a cooking basket along with hot coals.

Safety

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Dense stands can spread aggressively and crowd out surrounding vegetation if unmanaged.
