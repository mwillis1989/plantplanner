Common Camas

Camassia quamash · Asparagaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowWildflower (Forb)Zone 4a–8b

Overview

Summary

Perennial Forb, 20-60 cm height with purplish-blue (rarely light blue or white) flowers and edible bulb. Inhabiting mesic to vernally moist meadows and grasslands across lowland, steppe, and montane zones; locally frequent on southeastern Vancouver Island (with ssp. azurea rare in peatlands on western Vancouver Island), the Gulf Islands, and southeastern British Columbia; ranging east to Alberta and south to Montana, Idaho, and Washington.

Botanical Description

A showy perennial wildflower in the Asparagus plant family with purplish-blue flowers (sometimes light blue and occasionally white) and an edible bulb. Yellow, pollen-packed stamens contrast against the colourful tepals and the large, open flowers draw in a rich diversity of pollinators.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Inhabiting mesic to vernally moist meadows and grasslands across lowland, steppe, and montane zones; locally frequent on southeastern Vancouver Island (with ssp. azurea rare in peatlands on western Vancouver Island), the Gulf Islands, and southeastern British Columbia; ranging east to Alberta and south to Montana, Idaho, and Washington. Distributed across western North America from British Columbia to California, east to Alberta, Montana, Wyoming, and Utah, occupying mountain grasslands, prairies, wet meadows, and coastal forests from sea level to 2134 m, as well as coastal oak meadows. Rock outcrops. Great camas grows in wet meadows, woods, prairies, moist hillsides, and streamside areas.

Habitat Types

Forests; Meadows; Prairies; Rock Outcrops & Bluffs; Riparian & Streambanks

Biogeoclimatic Zones

Coastal Douglas-fir (CDFmm); Coastal Western Hemlock (CWHxm); Bunchgrass (BG); Ponderosa Pine (PP); Interior Douglas-fir (IDF)

BC Regional Range

Vancouver Island; Gulf Islands; Lower Mainland; Coast Mountains; Southern Interior; West of Cascade Crest; East of Cascade Crest

Elevation

Lowland to Steppe to Montane (0–1200 m)

Successional Role

Mid to Late-Successional (Inferred). Maintained by grassfires / Indian burning.

Fire Ecology

Late summer field burning (where permitted) improves stand vigor, reduces brush and weed competition, and aids regeneration; Indigenous camas tending included periodic post-harvest summer burning.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow; Rock Garden; Hedgerow / Thicket; Boulevard / Hellstrip

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Drought Tolerant; Erosion Control; Edible

Soil Preferences

Dry to seasonally wet; shallow dry soils or winter-flooded clay soils. Heavy clay / Seasonally wet clay. Tolerates very dry shallow soils as well as winter flooding in clay soils.

Moisture Notes

Adapted to well-drained winter-wet soils with prolonged summer dry periods.

Spread Behaviour

Slowly multiplying clump from underground bulbs or corms

Root Formation

Bulb / Corm / Tuber

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota endomycorrhizal network)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic and non-aggressive (Bulb / Corm / Tuber); low root competition makes it safe for intimate mixed borders and diverse polyculture interplanting.

Erosion & Slope Control

Slope & bank stabilization.

Fall & Winter Interest

Emerges winter-to-spring before retreating into summer dormancy.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Edible bulb (3.90 Calories, 1.76 mg Calcium, 0.13 g Protein, 0.23 mg Iron, 0.80 g Carbohydrate, 0.40 mg Magnesium per gram dry weight). Food (bulb). Staple root food high in protein/carbs.

Edible Parts & Flavour

Bulb; Staple root food high in protein/carbs; bulbs roasted in pit ovens 24-48 hrs (sweet like baked pears/molasses), made into loaves/cakes, boiled in stews, or dried; trade good; weeded out death camas; Edible bulb (3.90 Calories, 1.76 mg Calcium, 0.13 g Protein, 0.23 mg Iron, 0.80 g Carbohydrate, 0.40 mg Magnesium per gram dry weight); Bulbs.

Material & Technology

The bulbs were dug with a pointed digging stick.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Traditionally utilized by Nez Perce as cough medicine (boiled juice mixed with honey). Death camas used with extreme caution in a mix with other medicines. Gynecological aid to induce labor.

Propagation & Seeds

Stratification & Dormancy

Cold stratification required. Great camas can be propagated from seeds or bulbs; shade tolerance, moisture requirement, and preferred planting depth may differ among specific phenotypes.

Sowing Protocol

Sow in fall for spring germination.

Propagation Methods

Cultivars of Leichtlin’s camas are widely available in the flower bulb industry.
