Slough Sedge

Carex obnupta · Cyperaceae

Overview

Summary

Grass-like green rhizomatous perennial sedge, 30-120 cm height with fibrous bunching roots; dark brown unisexual inflorescences; small seed fruits. Occurs in swamps, marshes, riverbanks, wet meadows, and alluvial forests in the lowland zone; common in southern coastal British Columbia; south to California; an obligate wetland species inhabiting river and pond banks, ditches, coastal swamps, and marshes below 900 m elevation; tolerates saline water.

Botanical Description

Robust, grass-like green rhizomatous perennial sedge (Cyperaceae) with fibrous bunching roots, growing 30-150 cm tall (overall height range 30-120 cm). Perennial, densely tufted herb from long, creeping rhizomes and fibrous roots, the roots with soft, reddish-brown hairs; stems 30-150 cm tall, arising a few or many together, longer than the leaves. Stems arise a few or many together, exceeding the leaves. Sheaths are tight, cross-wrinkled, reddish- to brownish-tinged at the base, with the lowest ones breaking into threads; ligules are as long as wide and rounded.

Soil Preferences

It grows in wet, shallow, inundated woods, meadows, roadside ditches, coastal swamps, lakeshores, bogs, marshes, and riverbanks. In muddy areas it can grow as a dense, single-species stand.

Moisture Notes

Thrives in seasonally saturated soils, riparian benches, or poorly drained depressions.

Spread Behaviour

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic moderate clonal spreader (Rhizomatous); weaves steadily through mixed groundcover matrices without harsh chemical inhibition.

Pruning & Management

The leaves of slough sedge are cut in late summer (August). A knife was used to cut the plant at the base, as low to the ground as possible. The best plants were said to be those growing in 10 cm (4 in) of water. The cluster of leaves are cut off or pulled from the tender white bases, where they break off at or just below the ground level.

Fall & Winter Interest

Year-round evergreen foliage anchors winter garden structure.

Birds

Small seeds eaten by birds; Nesting cover for birds. Birds known to eat sedge seeds include coots, ducks (such as wood ducks, canvasbacks, mallards, pintails, teal, shoveler), marsh birds and shorebirds (dowichers, rails, and sandpipers), upland gamebirds (grouse, pheasant, and wild turkey), and songbirds (house finch, junco, sparrow, and towhee). Waterfowl and ducks eat sedge seeds frequently in small to fair amounts.

Mammals & Browse

River Otters, Beavers, Deer-resistant. Wetland shelter for otters and beavers; deer-resistant.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Material & Technology

The leaves of Carex obnupta, are used for both wrapping and twining in the "grass" baskets that are well known and widely marketed by Ditidaht (Nitinaht) and Nuu-chah-nulth (Nootka) women even today. "Grass" baskets are created using "three-cornered grass" (Scirpus pungens) for the basket bottom and ribs, slough sedge for the wrapping, and the top rim is made of the inner bark of western red cedar (Thuja plicata). Leaves used as basket-making twine (horizontal weave) to create fine, regular, tightly sealed baskets.
