Farewell-to-spring

Clarkia amoena · Onagraceae

Overview

Summary

Common native annual wildflower, 0.2–0.9 m tall; upright to spreading leafy stems, grayish-green; leaves alternate, linear to lance-shaped; flowers cup-shaped, pale pink to deep wine red/magenta with 4 notched petals, crimson/purplish center marking; dry pod-like capsule fruit. Inhabiting rocky slopes and dry grassy areas in the lowland zone, this taxon is infrequent on southern Vancouver Island and the adjacent Gulf Islands, extending south to CA; it also occupies relatively dry, open slopes, meadows, prairies, seaside bluffs, coastal scrub, forest edges, roadsides, and spring-wet areas from southern BC west of the Cascades to the central CA coast, including rocky slopes.

Botanical Description

A common native annual wildflower featuring striking large magenta to pink flowers that bloom later than most other spring annuals, displaying upright to spreading leafy grayish-green stems 0.2–0.9 m tall. Very drought-tolerant and best suited to very well-drained shallow soils in full sun. The seeds are tiny and must remain near the soil surface where the fall rains coincide with their germination.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow; Woodland / Shade; Rock Garden; Coastal / Bluff; Hedgerow / Thicket

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Drought Tolerant; Erosion Control; Edible

Soil Preferences

Dry open slopes, meadows, prairies, seaside bluffs, and forest clearings. Dry; well-drained shallow soils. Prefers well-drained to dry soil; tolerates alkaline, salt, sand, clay, and seaside conditions.

Moisture Notes

Adapted to well-drained winter-wet soils with prolonged summer dry periods.

Spread Behaviour

Deep-rooted non-spreading clump

Root Formation

Taproot

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota endomycorrhizal network)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic and non-aggressive (Taproot); low root competition makes it safe for intimate mixed borders and diverse polyculture interplanting.

Erosion & Slope Control

Drought-tolerant, easily established from seed for slope and bank stabilization; readily self-sows.

Propagation & Seeds

Stratification & Dormancy

Plants are easily established from seed by direct sowing on site in the fall or early spring. They can also be sown in flats of potting soil indoors and planted out at a spacing of 6 to 15 cm after the last frost date in the spring, but plants will generally be sturdier if direct-sown.

Sowing Protocol

Sow in fall or early spring for germination in the same season.

Propagation Methods

Direct-sow seed in fall or early spring on shallow, well-drained soils. Readily self-sows.
