Siberian Miner's Lettuce

Claytonia sibirica · Montiaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowWildflower (Forb)Zone 4a–8b🦋 4 Lepidoptera hosts

Overview

Summary

Perennial Forb (sometimes annual), 10-40 cm height with deep green lance to egg-shaped basal leaves, wine-colored stems, and small whiteish-pink flowers. Inhabiting forests, woodlands, moist shady meadows, beaches, thickets, and streambanks across lowland, steppe, and montane zones; common in and west of the Coast-Cascade Mountains south of 55°N, becoming less frequent eastward; an amphiberingian taxon extending south to Montana, Utah, and California, as well as East Asia, with a preference for sandy, acidic soils.

Botanical Description

A mostly perennial wildflower (sometimes annual) of rich, moist sites in shade to partial shade. Mature plants will produce ample seeds to create distinctive patches of small whiteish-pink flowers through most months of the year. Leaves are a deep, dark green and the stems can be a rich wine colour with the colours accenting each other. All parts of the plant are edible but can be bitter during and after flowering.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Food (leaves intended for salad). Edible leaves and stems raw or cooked as salads/greens (high Vit C).

Edible Parts & Flavour

Leaves (Salad Intended); Edible leaves and stems raw or cooked as salads/greens (high Vit C; steaming sweetens flavor).

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Hairwash made from cold infusion of stems (Cowlitz, Quileute, Skokomish, Snohomish). Stem juice used as eyewash, and plant infusion as a urinary aid (Quileute). Prepared as an eyewash and poultice for cuts and sores (Hesquiat). Edible green high in vitamin C eaten raw or cooked in salads/steamed to prevent scurvy (by miners).
