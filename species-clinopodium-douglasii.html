Yerba Buena

Overview

Summary

Found in mesic to dry open coniferous forests in the lowland, steppe, and montane zones; infrequent in southern British Columbia east of the Coast-Cascade Mountains, common on southern Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands, and infrequent on the adjacent mainland; distributed south to Idaho and California. Perennial trailing herb from a woody rhizome, mat-forming; stems reclining, to 1 m long, slightly woody, minutely sparse-hairy, 4-angled, sometimes rooting, often with short ascending branches; aromatic.

Botanical Description

Perennial trailing herb from a woody rhizome, mat-forming; stems reclining, to 1 m long, slightly woody, minutely sparse-hairy, 4-angled, sometimes rooting, often with short ascending branches; aromatic. Stems reclining, reaching to 1 m long, slightly woody, 4-angled, minutely sparse-hairy, sometimes rooting along their length, and frequently bearing short ascending branches. Leaves opposite, egg-shaped to almost round, 1-3.5 cm long, with rounded tips and irregularly blunt-toothed margins, minutely sparse-hairy, and short-stalked or nearly unstalked. Low trailing aromatic perennial herb with small rounded opposite leaves and tiny white/purple flowers.

Name Etymology

Genus Clinopodium; specific epithet douglasii honors a botanical collector or naturalist associated with Yerba Buena.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Leaves used as tea. Food (tea from leaves). Leaves gathered fresh or dried and steeped into a choice, refreshing herbal tea.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Traditionally utilized for digestive upset, stomach aches, and soothing fevers.
