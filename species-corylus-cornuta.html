Beaked Hazelnut

Corylus cornuta Marsh. · Betulaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowTree; ShrubZone 4a–8b🦋 38 Lepidoptera hosts

Overview

Summary

Shrub up to 3 m tall with smooth mottled brown/grey bark; alternate oval leaves with coarsely toothed margins; yellowish brown male catkins, scaly bud-like female catkins; round nut in bristly green flask-shaped beak. Produces nuts encased in a husk forming a beak-like shape, hanging in clusters of two or more 5.

Botanical Description

A deciduous shrub or small tree growing 1-8(-15) meters tall, rhizomatous and forming colonial thickets with smooth mottled brown/grey bark and straight main stems with spreading, ascending branches. Twigs are light brown, sparsely to moderately hairy, and sometimes glandular. Leaves are alternate, deciduous, nearly round to narrowly ovate or ovate-oblong, 4-10 cm long, with a heart-shaped or rounded base, blunt to broadly acute apex, often nearly angular and slightly lobed near the tip, and doubly serrate margins, usually pubescent on major veins and vein axils; petioles may bear glandular hairs. Male (staminate) and female (pistillate) flowers are separate, but both types present on each plant (the species monoecious); male flowers numerous in long, pendulous stalks (catkins) 4-6 cm long, in clusters of 2-3 near branch tips, appearing in the fall but opening the following spring; female flowers: several in a scaly bud, tiny and inconspicuous with only bright red stigma and styles protruding from the otherwise gray-brown buds, almost completely enclosed by bracts, near the end of the twigs.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Occurs in mesic sites across lowland and montane zones, featuring var. cornuta, which is common south of 57°N east of the Coast-Cascade Mountains, and var. californica, which is frequent on southern Vancouver Island and the lower Fraser Valley while becoming rare to the east. Ranges east to Newfoundland and south to Georgia (var. cornuta), and east to Idaho and south to California (var. californica). Inhabits thickets, open woods, and landscapes disturbed by fire or logging, growing in neutral to acidic soils (pH 4.8-7.5) from British Columbia to Newfoundland south to California and Georgia (29). Found in moist, well-drained sites, dry rocky areas, and meadows (5), with a strong preference for moist yet well-drained conditions.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow; Rock Garden; Rain Garden / Wetland; Hedgerow / Thicket

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Bird Forage; Deer Resistant; Edible

Soil Preferences

Thickets, open woods, forest margins, and burned/logged clearings; moist well-drained to dry neutral-to-acidic soils (pH 4.8–7.5). Rich, well-drained, loamy soils.

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Spread Behaviour

Rhizomatous colony- or thicket-former

Root Formation

Deep lateral / Taproot / Woody burl

Mycorrhizal Association

Ectomycorrhizal (EcM; Suillus, Russula, Lactarius, Amanita & forest truffle symbionts)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic but vigorous clonal spreader (Deep lateral / Taproot / Woody burl); forms thickets that outcompete slow-establishing forbs—allow generous spacing or use root barriers in tight borders.

Erosion & Slope Control

60% intact in AB oil sands; seeds collected by Oil Sands Vegetation Cooperative.

Pruning & Management

Beaked hazelnut is removed by growers of commercial, closely managed forests, primarily because of its aggressive colonial habit and corresponding competition with timber trees.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Edible nut. Nuts widely traded among Salishan and Columbia River peoples. Food (edible hazelnuts harvested).

Edible Parts & Flavour

Nuts; Edible nuts roasted, candied, in oil or flour; husks rotted away in ground for 10 days; Nut is collected for food when ready.

Material & Technology

Roots used for dye. Stems used in basketry. Prepared and applied to fashion bows, tools, fish traps, baby carriers.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Nut's milk used to cure cough and colds, heal cuts. Nut milk used for coughs, colds, and healing cuts. Infusion of branches as a blood purifier to treat heart problems and gastrointestinal disorders. Branch infusion drunk as a blood purifier to treat heart issues and gastrointestinal distress.
