Menzies' Larkspur

Delphinium menziesii D.C. · Ranunculaceae

Overview

Summary

A perennial forb and herb arising from tuberous roots, bearing deep-blue, bilaterally symmetric flowers on plants 20-60 cm in height. Inhabiting meadows, rocky slopes, and woodlands, as well as mesic to dry grassy bluffs, meadows, and forest openings in the lowland and lower montane zones, this species is common in southwestern British Columbia and extends south to Oregon, with a habitat presence also noted in meadows, rocky slopes, and woodlands.

Botanical Description

A perennial forb and herb arising from tuberous roots, bearing deep-blue, bilaterally symmetric flowers on plants 20-60 cm in height. Perennial herb from tuberous roots; stems slender, erect, simple or branched above, 35-70 cm tall, minutely-hairy, sometimes glandular, bases often reddish. At flowering time, there are none to 4 basal leaves and 3 to 7 alternate stem leaves with stalks 0.5-11 cm long. Leaf blades are round, 1.5-5 cm long and 3-9 cm wide, reduced upward, and palmately divided, with leaflets incised into narrowly oblong to linear segments.

Name Etymology

Genus Delphinium; specific epithet menziesii honors a botanical collector or naturalist associated with Menzies' Larkspur.
