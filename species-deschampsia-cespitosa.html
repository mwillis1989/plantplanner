Tufted Hairgrass

Deschampsia cespitosa · Poaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowGrass / Sedge / RushZone 1a–8b🦋 77 Lepidoptera hosts
🔎 Open 5-Tab Explorer Profile📊 Compare in Native Plant MatrixE-Flora BC ↗

Overview

Summary

Cool-season robust tufted perennial bunchgrass, 20 to 150 cm tall; shallow roots; rigid linear leaves (1.5-4 mm wide); loose open nodding panicle (10-25 cm); pale purplish sparkling spikelets; 2.5-3.5 mm grain. Saltmarshes, meadows, and shorelines.

Botanical Description

Long panicles of small flowers sparkle on elongated stalks in summer. Well-suited to, and characteristic of, saline environments, but can be incorporated into wetland plantings and even coastal slopes.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Saltmarshes, meadows, and shorelines. Saline meadows, beaches and tidal marshes in the lowland zone and moist meadows in the montane to alpine zones (ssp. beringensis ) and moist to mesic meadows, lakes shores, rocky ridges, shrub-carrs and talus slopes in the montane to alpine zones (ssp. cespitosa ); ssp. beringensis - common along the coast, ssp. cespitosa - common in and E of the Coast-Cascade Mountains in BC; ssp. beringensis - amphiberingian, N to AK and S to N WA; ssp. cespitosa - circumpolar, N to AK, YT and NT, E to NF and S to ME, MA, PA, NC, KY, IL, MN, SD, NM, AZ, and CA. Subspecies beringensis is common along the coast, whereas ssp. cespitosa is common in and east of the Coast-Cascade Mountains in British Columbia. Subspecies beringensis is amphiberingian, ranging north to Alaska and south to northern Washington; ssp. cespitosa is circumpolar, ranging north to Alaska, Yukon Territory, and Northwest Territories, east to Newfoundland, and south to Maine, Massachusetts, Pennsylvania, North Carolina, Kentucky, Illinois, Minnesota, South Dakota, New Mexico, Arizona, and California, with occurrences in Greenland and Eurasia.

Habitat Types

Biogeoclimatic Zones

BC Regional Range

Vancouver Island; Gulf Islands; Lower Mainland; Coast Mountains; Southern Interior; West of Cascade Crest; East of Cascade Crest

Elevation

Lowland to Montane to Subalpine (0–2200 m)

Successional Role

Mid-Successional / Wetland (Inferred). Early to mid seral.

Fire Ecology

Defer grazing each spring until soils are dry enough to prevent damage to the soils and plants and defer grazing at least one year after wildfire to allow recovery (Walsh, 1995), (Darris and Gonzalves, 2009). U.S. Department of Agriculture, Forest Service, Rocky Mountain Research Station, Fire Sciences Laboratory.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow; Rock Garden; Rain Garden / Wetland; Coastal / Bluff; Hedgerow / Thicket

Key Features

Butterfly / Moth Host; Deer Resistant; Salt Tolerant; Winter Interest; Edible

Soil Preferences

Moist open areas, salt marshes, high-elevation bogs, and rocky shores; wide soil pH tolerance (3.7–8.3). Moist to wet, seasonally flooded; saline tolerant. A wide range of soil conditions with pH 3.7 to 8.3; adapts to colonize calcareous mine waste, acidified soils, heavy metal contaminated soils, and low nutrient soils.

Moisture Notes

Thrives in seasonally saturated soils, riparian benches, or poorly drained depressions.

Spread Behaviour

Tufted non-spreading clump

Root Formation

Fibrous

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota endomycorrhizal network)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic and non-aggressive (Fibrous); low root competition makes it safe for intimate mixed borders and diverse polyculture interplanting.

Erosion & Slope Control

Soil stabilization on disturbed areas, coal mine spoils, calcareous/acidic mine waste; heavy metal tolerant (zinc); well-suited for wetland, estuary, and wet meadow plantings.

Fall & Winter Interest

Semi-evergreen

Propagation & Seeds

Seed Collection

Tufted hairgrass has approximately 1,300,000 seeds per pound (PLANTS Database).

Sowing Protocol

Sow in fall or early spring for germination in the same season. The recommended full seeding rate is 1.5 pounds pure live seed (PLS) per acre.

Propagation Methods

Propagated by wind-dispersed seed; fast germination (3–5 days). Tolerates heavy metal contamination and acid soils.
