Overview

Summary

Semi-evergreen Perennial Bunchgrass, 20-80 cm height with blueish-green blades. Inhabiting meadows, rock outcrops, and xeric coastal meadows.

Botanical Description

A showy, medium-height, drought-tolerant, semi-evergreen bunchgrass reaching 20-80 cm in height, characterized by striking bluish-green blades suited for full sun exposure and serving as a host plant for local butterfly oviposition. Native semi-evergreen perennial bunchgrass (20–80 cm tall) with fine bluish-green foliage. Semi-evergreen perennial bunchgrass (20-80 cm) with blueish-green blades.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Inhabiting meadows, rock outcrops, and xeric coastal meadows. The range of Idaho fescue extends to California, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Nevada, Oregon, South Dakota, Utah, Washington, and Wyoming. Idaho fescue is one of the most common and widely distributed grasses in the Western States.

Habitat Types

Meadows; Rock Outcrops & Bluffs

Biogeoclimatic Zones

BC Regional Range

Elevation

Lowland to Montane (0–1200 m)

Successional Role

Mid-Successional (Inferred). Climax meadow grass.

Fire Ecology

Following fire, protect Idaho fescue from grazing for two full growing seasons. An additional year's deferment may be needed to achieve full plant recovery, and to re-establish a suitable stubble height because palatability increases significantly following fire.

Propagation & Seeds

Stratification & Dormancy

Natural establishment: Idaho fescue produces a fair amount of seed of comparatively high viability and maintains itself well on the range if given a reasonable opportunity (USDA, Forest Service, 1988).

Sowing Protocol

Sow in fall or early spring for germination in the same season. One PLS pound of Idaho fescue seed contains approximately 425,000 to 460,000 seeds and a broadcast planting of one pound of Idaho fescue seed results in a seeding distribution of approximately 10.1 seeds per square foot (USDA, NRCS, 1996).

Propagation Methods

Sow seeds in fall or early spring. Core native bunchgrass.
