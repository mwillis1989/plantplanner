Overview

Summary

Stoloniferous Perennial Forb (5-20 cm) with shiny evergreen foliage, mat-forming cover, white flowers, creeping runners, and small red or white sweet berries. Thriving on shorelines and beaches, as well as dry to mesic sand dunes and rocky coastal bluffs just above high tide in the lowland zone, this plant is frequent in coastal British Columbia, ranging north to Alaska and south to California, with disjunct populations in South America; additionally found in sunny dry sites below 200 m elevation in dune and grassland communities of coastal California, spanning from Alaska to coastal South America and Hawaii.

Botanical Description

Shiny evergreen foliage for the salt-spray zone and tasty small red or white berries make this species desirable. Spreads by stolons, for mat-forming cover for coastal rocky slopes and meadows. A stoloniferous perennial forb 5-20 cm tall valued for its shiny evergreen foliage in salt-spray zones and its small, sweet red or white berries. It forms mat-like cover on coastal rocky slopes and meadows via creeping runners.

Name Etymology

Genus Fragaria; geographic specific epithet chiloensis denotes the plant's North American regional distribution.

Erosion & Slope Control

Groundcover that thrives without irrigation; Slope & bank stabilization.

Pruning & Management

Keep the runners pruned back because they can be invasive. It is necessary to divide the patch every three to four years and start a new patch for increased vigor. Younger plants are more vigorous and produce more berries.

Fall & Winter Interest

Year-round evergreen foliage anchors winter garden structure.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

It was gathered and eaten raw by the Makah, Quileute, and Quinault of western Washington. Food (fruits & leaves for tea). Fruits & Leaves (Both Tea Intended).

Edible Parts & Flavour

Fruits & Leaves (Both Tea Intended); Edible sweet berries and leaves (intended for tea); Fruits and leaves (tea).

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

The plant is also used medicinally by the Quileute by chewing the leaves and spitting them on burns.

Propagation & Seeds

Sowing Protocol

Dig up plantlets or runners and plant them in pots in summer. Be sure to cover the stems and roots with soil. Place the pots in a hothouse to establish good, strong roots.

Propagation Methods

Seeds and plants of selected Fragaria cultivars are available from many nurseries. It is best to plant species from your local area, adapted to the specific site conditions where the plants are to be grown.
