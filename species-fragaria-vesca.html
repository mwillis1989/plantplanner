Overview

Summary

A stoloniferous perennial forb with a low trailing habit, trifoliate ovate toothed green leaves, white 5-petaled flowers, and small aromatic red strawberries (with beach plants bearing waxy leaves). Found in woodlands and forests, specifically in dry to moist open forests, sandy fields, clearings, and meadows from the lowland to subalpine zones; common in southern British Columbia south of 53°N, rare northward; ranging north to Northwest Territories, east to Newfoundland, and south to California, New Mexico, Missouri, and Virginia, as well as shrubby areas, seashores, and sandy or rocky seashores.

Botanical Description

A stoloniferous perennial forb with a low trailing habit, trifoliate ovate toothed green leaves, white 5-petaled flowers, and small aromatic red strawberries (with beach plants bearing waxy leaves). Perennial herb from a fibrous root and short, thick rhizome, with long, leafless runners; stems trailing, rooting at the nodes; runners, leaf stalks and flower shoots greenish or very lightly tinged with reddish purple, lightly to densely hairy. The runners, leaf stalks, and flower shoots are greenish or very lightly tinged with reddish-purple, and are lightly to densely hairy. Basal leaves are arranged in rosettes, are palmately compound on stalks 3-12 cm long, and bear 3 elliptic to egg-shaped, more or less unstalked leaflets measuring 1-3 cm long.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Found in woodlands and forests, specifically in dry to moist open forests, sandy fields, clearings, and meadows from the lowland to subalpine zones; common in southern British Columbia south of 53°N, rare northward; ranging north to Northwest Territories, east to Newfoundland, and south to California, New Mexico, Missouri, and Virginia, as well as shrubby areas, seashores, and sandy or rocky seashores. Found below 2000 m in partial shade of closed-cone pine, evergreen, mixed conifer forests, and chaparral across a very wide distribution.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow; Woodland / Shade; Rock Garden; Coastal / Bluff; Hedgerow / Thicket

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Winter Interest; Edible

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Spread Behaviour

Creeping mat spreading via aboveground stolons

Root Formation

Stoloniferous

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota endomycorrhizal network)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic moderate clonal spreader (Stoloniferous); weaves steadily through mixed groundcover matrices without harsh chemical inhibition.

Pruning & Management

Keep the runners pruned back because they can be invasive. It is necessary to divide the patch every 3 to 4 years and start a new patch for increased vigor. Younger plants are more vigorous and produce more berries.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Fruit edible but not abundant. The fruit was eaten raw and not preserved by California Indian tribes including the Coast Yuki and the Karok. Food (fruits & leaves for tea). Berries eaten raw or used in dishes and jams.

Edible Parts & Flavour

Fruits & Leaves (Both Tea Intended); Edible berries eaten raw, in desserts, jams; fresh/dried leaves brewed into tea for anti-diarrhea properties.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Leaves brewed to make tea (Halkomelem, shíshálh (Sechelt), Cowlitz, Micmac) and used for anti-diarrhea properties. Fresh/dried leaves brewed into tea for anti-diarrhea properties. Leaves brewed for medicinal tea.

Safety

Edibility

Yes

Pets, Children & Livestock

Generally non-toxic; harvest away from roadsides and pesticides, and confirm species ID before consumption.

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Recorded insect/host associations (beees sheet): Argyrotaenia franciscana: in tied flowers/leaves; Chloroclysta citrata: on leaves; Diaphania hyalinata: on fruits/leaves/stems; Ectoedemia arcuatella: in leaves; Elasmopalpus lignosellus: on fruits; Hypercompe indecisa: on fruits/leaves; Incurvaria praelatella: in leaves; Mesoleuca albicillata: on leaves

Propagation & Seeds

Propagation Methods

Dig up plantlets or runners and pot them in summer, covering stems and roots with soil; place pots in a warm frame or greenhouse to establish strong roots.

Seeding Rate (seeds/m²)
