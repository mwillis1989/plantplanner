Cascara

Overview

Summary

Deciduous Tree up to 8 m height with dark green deeply veined leaves, smooth grayish-brown bark, greenish-yellow flowers, and blueish-black drupes. Found in thickets and woodlands; inhabiting moist to mesic streambanks, thickets, woodlands, and forests in the lowland, steppe, and montane zones; common in southwestern British Columbia, frequent in south-central and southeastern British Columbia south of 51°N; ranging south to Idaho, Maryland, and California; thriving in moist, wet, disturbed sites under partial sun, including riparian zones and shaded mixed or coniferous forests.

Botanical Description

The dark green and deeply veined foliage of this small deciduous tree is as notable as its blueish-black plump fruits in summer. In late spring to early summer, the plant produces small, inconspicuous greenish-yellow flowers that develop into plump, blueish-black drupes (berries) in summer, serving as an important late-summer and fall food source for birds such as thrushes, waxwings, and robins, while the foliage is consumed by Pale Swallowtail butterfly caterpillars. A deciduous shrub or small tree up to 10 m tall (also noted as 8-10 m tall), featuring yellow- to brownish hairs and silvery-grey bark that is characteristically smooth and grayish-brown when young, darkening and becoming scaly with age.

Name Etymology

Genus Frangula; species Frangula purshiana.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Aged bark boiled for tea/medicine (strong laxative tonic). Prepared as medicine (strong laxative) alone or mixed with other ingredients. Medicine (laxative bark preparations), D-adze handles.
