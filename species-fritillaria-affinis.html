Chocolate Lily

Overview

Summary

Perennial Forb from bulb, 20-60 cm height with bell-shaped brownish-purple/yellowish-green mottled flowers; root bulblets look like rice; flowers smell like raw meat. Found in meadows and woodlands; inhabiting dry to mesic grassy bluffs, meadows, and open forests in the lowland and steppe to lower subalpine zones; common on southeastern Vancouver Island, the Gulf Islands, and inland to south-central British Columbia south of 52°N; ranging south to Idaho and California.

Botanical Description

Belonging to the lily family (Liliaceae), the nodding flowers are bell-shaped to bowl-shaped, possessing a scent like raw meat and variable petals/six tepals ranging from brownish-purple mottled with greenish-yellow to yellowish-green mottled with purple (or brown-mottled with green or yellow). A perennial herb and forb growing from a bulb composed of a few larger, fleshy scales and numerous small, rice-grain bulblets (with root bulblets resembling rice), reaching 20-80 cm tall (or 20-60 cm height, and 20-50 cm in chocolate lily). Perennial herb from a bulb of a few larger, fleshy scales and numerous small, rice-grain bulblets; stems 20-80 cm tall, slender to sturdy, smooth. Leaves occur usually in 1 or 2 whorls of 3 to 5 (or 1-3 whorls of 5-11) on the stem, scattered and alternate above, narrowly lanceolate to lance-egg-shaped, measuring 5-10 (15) cm long and 0.5-3 cm wide, and smooth.

Name Etymology

Genus Fritillaria; species Fritillaria affinis.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Found in meadows and woodlands; inhabiting dry to mesic grassy bluffs, meadows, and open forests in the lowland and steppe to lower subalpine zones; common on southeastern Vancouver Island, the Gulf Islands, and inland to south-central British Columbia south of 52°N; ranging south to Idaho and California. Chocolate lily occurs in open dry woods and meadows from southern coastal British Columbia eastward to the Okanagan in eastern Washington and south to California. In California, chocolate lily occurs commonly in oak or pine scrub and grasslands below 1800 m in the Klamath Ranges, north Coast Ranges, Cascade, northern Sierra Nevada Foothills, San Francisco Bay area (Hickman 1993).
