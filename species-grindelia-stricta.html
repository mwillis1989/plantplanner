Overview

Summary

A sun-loving, drought-tolerant, deer-resistant perennial forb 30-80 cm (or 15-80 cm) in height, well-suited for well-drained rocky sites and tending toward a fuller stature in deeper, richer soils; it produces cheerful yellow flowers over a long, late-season bloom period, attracting an abundant diversity of native pollinators. Inhabiting mesic to dry beaches, coastal bluffs, waste places, and roadsides in the lowland zone, this plant is common in coastal British Columbia and ranges south to northern California.

Botanical Description

This plant blooms over a long period of time and late into the season, attracting a large diversity and abundance of native pollinators. Tends to be a full-statured plant that will get larger in deeper and richer soils. Perennial herb from a taproot and a stout branched stem-base; stems erect or ascending, several, usually branched, nearly glabrous or with long, soft hairs, 15-80 cm tall.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow; Rock Garden; Rain Garden / Wetland; Coastal / Bluff; Hedgerow / Thicket

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Deer Resistant; Drought Tolerant; Salt Tolerant; Erosion Control

Soil Preferences

Coastal bluffs, beaches, and salt marshes. Dry to mesic; well-drained rocky or rich soils.

Moisture Notes

Adapted to well-drained winter-wet soils with prolonged summer dry periods.

Spread Behaviour

Deep-rooted non-spreading clump

Root Formation

Taproot

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota endomycorrhizal network)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic and non-aggressive (Taproot); low root competition makes it safe for intimate mixed borders and diverse polyculture interplanting.

Erosion & Slope Control

Slope & bank stabilization.

Fall & Winter Interest
