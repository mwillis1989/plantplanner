Oceanspray

Holodiscus discolor (Pursh.) Maxim. · Rosaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowShrubZone 4a–8b🦋 33 Lepidoptera hosts

Overview

Summary

Medium to tall deciduous shrub up to 4 m tall with multiple upright, arching stems, pithy/ridged stems, grayish-green shallowly-toothed leaves, and sweet-scented creamy-white lilac-like flower plumes; extremely hard wood. Thriving in forests, woodlands, thickets, and rocky slopes—as well as dry to mesic bluffs, clearings, forest edges, and open forests spanning the lowland to montane zones—this plant is common in southern British Columbia with a disjunct population in the Bella Coola valley, and ranges south to Idaho, Montana, and California.

Botanical Description

Drought-tolerant deciduous shrub up to 4 m with showy cascading white flowers. A drought-tolerant, medium to tall deciduous flowering shrub reaching 1–4 m in height, featuring extremely hard wood, multiple upright, arching, pithy/ridged, and hairy stems; young stems are ridged, while older stems bear reddish-grey, peeling bark. The cascading, showy, sweet-scented creamy-white lilac-like flower plumes bloom from June through July, supporting wildlife including nesting Bushtits, seed-foraging birds, and butterflies ovipositing on buds and leaves.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Thriving in forests, woodlands, thickets, and rocky slopes—as well as dry to mesic bluffs, clearings, forest edges, and open forests spanning the lowland to montane zones—this plant is common in southern British Columbia with a disjunct population in the Bella Coola valley, and ranges south to Idaho, Montana, and California. This species naturally establishes across shaded or sunny forests, along stream banks, on rocky slopes, and on freeway edges (0–2134 m), as well as in coastal regions and dry to moderately dry open forests, ocean shores, and rocky bluffs.

Successional Role

Deciduous, Deciduous shrub, Pioneer understory species. Pseudotsuga forest pioneer stages.

Fire Ecology

Pioneer species returned to stands of considerable age following fires; maintains pioneer understory in dry sites due to frequent smaller fires. Returned to stands of considerable age by fires of non-devastating extent; secondary pioneer stage.

Mycorrhizal Association

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic and non-aggressive (Fibrous); low root competition makes it safe for intimate mixed borders and diverse polyculture interplanting.

Erosion & Slope Control

Erosion control due to shallow adaptable root system; thrives in poor soils, drought, and salt spray; easily propagated.

Fall & Winter Interest

Distinctive winter bark and stem colour provide strong cold-season architectural interest.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Material & Technology

Hard, fire-resistant wood used for eating tongs, hunting arrows, fishing hooks, digging sticks, tools, needles, barbecue skewers, cradle/rattle/drum hoops, children's toys/bows, and teepee post holders.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Bark decoction used as eyewash (Makah, Lummi). Blossom tea used for diarrhea (Lummi). Dried fruiting heads steeped in boiling water as tea to alleviate diarrhoea (especially in children/infants). Seed infusion used as blood purifier and treatment for smallpox, black measles, and chicken pox (Chehalis). Leaf poultice used to cure sore lips/feet and purify blood (Squaxin).

Safety

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Recorded insect/host associations (beees sheet): Coleophora sp. C: in case on leaves; Epinotia johnsonana: in folded leaf; Eriocrania semipurpurella: in leaves; Incisalia augustinus: on flowers/fruits/leaves; Paonias excaecata: on leaves; Phyllonorycter holodisci: in leaves

Propagation & Seeds

Seed Collection

There are more than 5,000,000 seeds per pound.

Stratification & Dormancy

Oceanspray is typically propagated by seed requiring 15 to 18 weeks of cold moist storage (stratification) to overcome dormancy.

Propagation Methods

Propagates from seed, semi-hardwood cuttings, or suckers. Resprouts vigorously after coppicing or burning.
