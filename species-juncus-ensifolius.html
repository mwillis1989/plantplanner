Dagger-leaf Rush

Overview

Summary

Featuring unique iris-like foliage prized alongside its dark brown, globe-shaped terminal flower clusters 1 cm in diameter, this perennial rush grows 20-40 cm in height. Inhabiting meadows and wetlands, specifically wet, open, sandy soil in marshes, peat bogs, lakeshores, and margins of creeks and rivers, from the lowland and steppe to subalpine zones; frequent in British Columbia south of 55°N, infrequent to the north; amphiberingian, ranging north to Alaska and Yukon, east to Saskatchewan, disjunct to Ontario and Quebec, and south to Colorado, Arizona, and California; also found in East Asia.

Botanical Description

Featuring unique iris-like foliage prized alongside its dark brown, globe-shaped terminal flower clusters 1 cm in diameter, this perennial rush grows 20-40 cm in height. It is a perennial herb arising from long rhizomes with erect stems 30-60 cm tall. Leaves are located on the lower half of the stem, strongly flattened, laterally compressed (iris-like), and equipped with partial cross-walls extending from the edges to about 2/3 of the leaf width; sheaths are open, bearing short, ear-shaped lobes or lacking them entirely. Perennial rush (20–40 cm tall) with flattened iris-like leaves and dark head-like flower clusters.
