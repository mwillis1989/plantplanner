Western Rush

Juncus occidentalis · Juncaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowGrass / Sedge / RushZone 4a–8b🦋 42 Lepidoptera hosts
🔎 Open 5-Tab Explorer Profile📊 Compare in Native Plant MatrixE-Flora BC ↗

Overview

Summary

A short-statured perennial and deer-resistant rush adapted for meadow-like habitats in full sun; a tufted perennial rush reaching 15 – 60 cm in height with brown flowers.

Botanical Description

Tufted perennial rush (15–60 cm tall) with wiry stems and brownish terminal flower clusters.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Vernally wet meadows, seepage slopes, ditches, pond margins, and moist open prairies at low elevations.

Habitat Types

Meadows; Wetlands & Marshes

Biogeoclimatic Zones

Coastal Western Hemlock (CWH); Interior Douglas-fir (IDF); Interior Cedar – Hemlock (ICH)

BC Regional Range

Vancouver Island; Gulf Islands; Lower Mainland; Coast Mountains; Southern Interior; West of Cascade Crest; East of Cascade Crest

Elevation

Lowland to Montane (0–1200 m)

Successional Role

Mid-Successional (Inferred).

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow; Rain Garden / Wetland

Key Features

Butterfly / Moth Host; Deer Resistant

Soil Preferences

Seasonally wet meadows that dry out in summer. Wet winter and dry summer.

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Spread Behaviour

Tufted non-spreading clump

Root Formation

Fibrous

Mycorrhizal Association

Typically Non-Mycorrhizal or facultatively weakly AM (specialized root hairs or wetland nutrient uptake)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic and non-aggressive (Fibrous); low root competition makes it safe for intimate mixed borders and diverse polyculture interplanting.

Fall & Winter Interest

Year-round evergreen foliage anchors winter garden structure.
