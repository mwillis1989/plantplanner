Junegrass

Overview

Summary

Native cool-season tufted perennial bunchgrass, 30-60 cm tall; soft blue-green flat/in-rolled basal leaves; dense narrow contracted panicle seedheads with pale green/purplish spikelets. Dry rocky or grassy slopes and forest openings in the steppe to subalpine zones; common in SC and SE BC, less frequent in SW and N BC; circumpolar, N to AK, YT and SW NT, E to Labr. And S to ME, NY, OH, AL, MS, TX, NM, AZ, CA and MX.

Botanical Description

Native cool-season tufted perennial bunchgrass, 30-60 cm tall; soft blue-green flat/in-rolled basal leaves; dense narrow contracted panicle seedheads with pale green/purplish spikelets. Plant in dry soils for best results. A strong, tufted perennial bunchgrass arising from fibrous roots with smooth to soft-hairy stems measuring (20) 30-60 cm tall. Sheath margins are typically non-overlapping above, with collars frequently bearing long, straight hairs 1-1.5 mm long.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Dry rocky or grassy slopes and forest openings in the steppe to subalpine zones; common in SC and SE BC, less frequent in SW and N BC; circumpolar, N to AK, YT and SW NT, E to Labr. And S to ME, NY, OH, AL, MS, TX, NM, AZ, CA and MX. Common in south-central and southeastern British Columbia, less frequent in southwestern and northern British Columbia. Circumpolar, ranging north to Alaska, Yukon Territory, and southwestern Northwest Territories, east to Labrador, and south to Maine, New York, Ohio, Alabama, Mississippi, Texas, New Mexico, Arizona, California, and Mexico; also found in Eurasia. Thrives in rangeland meadows, plains, mountain foothills, and open forestlands at elevations of 1219–3505 m with 12-20 in precipitation, preferring well-drained silts, loams, and sandy loams with a pH of 6.5-8.0. It is most commonly found at elevations from 1219–2438 m, but occurs up to 3505 m in the central Rocky Mountains.

Fire Ecology

Prairie junegrass tends to increase in percent cover following fire events and is very resistant to fire. At least partially due to its small stature, prairie Junegrass is very resistant to fire and will often increase in percent cover after a fire or other disturbance.

Pruning & Management

During the second to third growing season, seedheads will flower in mid-April to mid-June. Seed reaches maturity in July through August depending on latitude and elevation. Fall regrowth can occur if soil moisture conditions are favorable. Prairie junegrass greens up early in the spring.

Fall & Winter Interest

Year-round evergreen foliage anchors winter garden structure.

Safety

Edibility

Yes

Pets, Children & Livestock

Generally non-toxic; harvest away from roadsides and pesticides, and confirm species ID before consumption.

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Prairie junegrass may be vulnerable to some insects such as the striped flea beetle which will cause the leaf tips to wilt. Prairie junegrass is medium-lived native species, spreading primarily via seed.

Propagation & Seeds

Seed Collection

There are about 1.8 million seeds per pound (+/- 20%) although viability is often below 50 percent.

Stratification & Dormancy

Prairie Junegrass seed has little dormancy following cold storage through winter and is generally planted in spring.

Sowing Protocol

Prairie junegrass is not recommended for fall or dormant fall seedings. It does best when seeded in the spring. Young plants are subject to thinning by frost heaving and soil cracking during winter and very early spring.

Propagation Methods

‘Barkoel’ prairie junegrass is the only release of this species. It originates from Barenbrug, Holland, Oosterhout, The Netherlands.
