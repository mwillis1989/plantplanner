Overview

Summary

The Columbia tiger lily (Lilium columbianum) is a perennial herb arising from an egg-shaped, fleshy-scaled, white bulb 3-5 cm long, featuring sturdy, smooth stems 40-120 cm tall (reaching 0.6–1.2 m or 60-120 cm in height) that produce bright orange, downward-facing, nodding flowers with dark spots. Inhabiting mesic open forests, roadsides, clearings, thickets, and meadows across lowland, steppe, and alpine zones; common throughout southern British Columbia south of 54°N; extending south to Idaho and northern California; also found in forests, meadows, and clearings from low to subalpine elevations, as well as in cultivated gardens in the San Juan and Gulf Islands.

Botanical Description

The Columbia tiger lily, Lilium columbianum, is a distinctive BC native lily that resembles turk’s cap and martagon lilies. Native lily (0.6–1.2 m) with slender stems producing small downward-facing light orange flowers with dark spots and reflexed petals. Mature bulbs can produce candelabras of up to 20 blooms. Tall perennial lily with nodding, spotted orange flowers and edible scaly white bulbs.

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Edible leaves, buds, and flowers. Bulbs harvested and eaten (especially when in bloom). Edible bulbs used by First Nations as a bitter/peppery flavouring for soups, meats, fish.

Edible Parts & Flavour

Edible bulbs used by First Nations as a bitter/peppery flavouring for soups, meats, fish.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Bulbs eaten in soup as a medicinal restorative. Medicine.
