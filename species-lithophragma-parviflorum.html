Small-flowered Woodland-star

Overview

Summary

A rhizomatous perennial forb standing 10-30 cm in height, characterized by pinkish-white flowers with uniquely divided petals. Occupying meadows, rocky slopes, and woodlands, as well as mesic to dry coastal bluffs, open coniferous and deciduous forests, grasslands, and shrublands across lowland, steppe, and montane zones, this plant is frequent in extreme southern British Columbia, ranging east to Alberta and south to Nebraska, Colorado, Utah, Nevada, and California.

Botanical Description

A rhizomatous perennial forb standing 10-30 cm in height, characterized by pinkish-white flowers with uniquely divided petals. This perennial herb arises from slender rhizomes with small bulblets, featuring erect stems 10-30 cm tall that are densely glandular-hairy throughout and noticeably purplish above. The basal leaves are round to kidney-shaped, 1-3 cm wide, palmately 3- to 5-divided nearly to the base and then again 2- to 3-times lobed, with more or less pointed teeth; their slender stalks measure 2-6 cm long and feature widened stipular bases. The stem leaves number 2 (rarely 1-3), are more divided than the basal leaves, and are unstalked above.

Name Etymology

Genus Lithophragma; specific epithet parviflorum honors a botanical collector or naturalist associated with Small-flowered Woodland.
