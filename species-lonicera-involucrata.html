Black Twinberry

Lonicera involucrata Banks ex Spreng. · Caprifoliaceae

Overview

Summary

A large deciduous shrub up to 5 m tall featuring yellow tubular flowers, red involucral bracts, and shiny paired black fruits. Found in riparian areas, thickets, and woodlands, as well as moist forests and thickets in the lowland, steppe, and montane zones; common in British Columbia south of 58°N; north to the Yukon Territory, east to Quebec, and south to Michigan, Wisconsin, New Mexico, California, and Mexico; inhabiting riparian areas, thickets, and woodlands.

Botanical Description

Yellow tubular flowers on this large deciduous shrub attract bees and hummingbirds. The involucral bracts are a showy red and have inspired the species name and shiny black fruits in pairs are savoured by waxwings and other birds, but not fit for human consumption. Natural Habitat

Found in riparian areas, thickets, and woodlands, as well as moist forests and thickets in the lowland, steppe, and montane zones; common in British Columbia south of 58°N; north to the Yukon Territory, east to Quebec, and south to Michigan, Wisconsin, New Mexico, California, and Mexico; inhabiting riparian areas, thickets, and woodlands. Lonicera involucrata is widely distributed across North America from Quebec westward to Alaska and British Columbia and southward into California, Colorado, Utah, and Arizona (McMinn 1939).

Soil Preferences

Twinberry honeysuckle is typically found in moist forests, clearings, riparian habitats, swamps and thickets (MacKinnon, Pojar & Coupe′ 1992). It prefers a good moist soil but can succeed in any fertile soil.

Moisture Notes

Thrives in seasonally saturated soils, riparian benches, or poorly drained depressions.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Edible Parts & Flavour

Inedible / Toxic.

Material & Technology

Berries used as dye. The Quileute and Kwakwaka’wakw people used these berries as a black pigment. The Haida rubbed the berries on the scalp to prevent hair from turning gray.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

A decoction of the bark has been applied to a woman’s breasts to encourage milk flow. An infusion was used to treat chest and stomach complaints and to cleanse the body. The Dakelh (Carrier) boiled the leaves and used the liquid to bathe sore eyes, or applied the crushed leaves as a poultice to open sores.

Safety

Edibility

No / Toxic

Pets, Children & Livestock

Keep showy fruits out of reach of young children; mildly irritating if ingested in quantity.

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Twinberry honeysuckle berries has been noted as poisonous. Since the European species of honeysuckle are regarded as emetic and cathartic, they are better avoided in favor of something less dubious. Berries regarded as inedible or toxic by humans ("raven's berry", "monster's fruit"). | Recorded insect/host associations (beees sheet): Euphydryas gillettii: on leaves

Propagation & Seeds

Stratification & Dormancy

Seed is obtained by collecting the ripe berries from July through September then macerating the fruit to separate the seed from the pulp. The seed should be cold moist stratified ( moist chilled at 1–2°C) for 30 to 90 days in order to overcome embryo dormancy and improve germination.

Sowing Protocol

Sow the seeds as soon as they are ripe in a cold frame. When the seedlings are large enough, plant them directly into their permanent positions in late spring or early summer.

Propagation Methods

Lonicera involucrata seeds should be collected from isolated plants and extracted by maceration in water.
