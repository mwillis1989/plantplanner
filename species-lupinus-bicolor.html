Bicoloured Lupine

Lupinus bicolor · Fabaceae

Overview

Summary

Small annual lupine with relatively large flowers compared to the size of the plant, requiring considerable drainage and found in gravel and shallow-soil meadows in full sun (slugs can be problematic for this species); annual forb, 10-20 cm in height with purple-blue and white flowers; annual herb from a taproot, with several more or less erect, branched stems 10-40 cm tall, bearing short, appressed or spreading-ascending hairs as well as scattered longer hairs; leaves alternate, palmately compound with 5 to 8 linear-oblanceolate leaflets that are sharp-pointed or blunt at the tip, 1.5-3 cm long, and appressed-hairy on both surfaces. Occupying rock outcrops, meadows, and shorelines, as well as dry to mesic meadows, grassy bluffs, sandy clearings, and forest glades in the lowland zone; infrequent on southeastern Vancouver Island, the Gulf Islands, and the lower mainland, extending south to California; inhabiting elevations from 0 to 2260 m with a pH range of 5 to 8.3 and a minimum soil depth of 5.1 cm.

Botanical Description

One of our small annual lupines with relatively large flowers compared to the size of the plant. Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow; Rock Garden; Coastal / Bluff; Hedgerow / Thicket; Boulevard / Hellstrip

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Drought Tolerant; Nitrogen Fixer; Erosion Control

Soil Preferences

Dry sandy meadows, bluffs, and shallow soils. Sunny dry bluffs, gravelly slopes, and open meadows. Dry; requires considerable drainage, shallow soils/gravel; pH 5 to 8.3.

Moisture Notes

Adapted to well-drained winter-wet soils with prolonged summer dry periods.

Spread Behaviour

Deep-rooted non-spreading clump

Root Formation

Taproot

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM) & Rhizobial Nitrogen-Fixing (Bradyrhizobium / Rhizobium nodules)

Competition / Allelopathy

Symbiotic nitrogen-fixer that elevates site fertility and accelerates canopy closure; in low-nutrient Garry oak or alpine meadows, space carefully so vigorous nitrogen responders do not crowd slow forbs.

Erosion & Slope Control

Slope & bank stabilization.

Pets, Children & Livestock

Seeds toxic to livestock (particularly sheep/cattle) and pets when ingested raw.

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Seeds and foliage contain quinolizidine alkaloids potentially toxic to livestock and pets if ingested raw in quantity. Young foliage and seedlings are susceptible to slug damage. | Recorded insect/host associations (beees sheet): Everes comyntas: on flowers/fruits/leaves
