Small-flowered Lupine

Lupinus polycarpus · Fabaceae

Overview

Summary

A small annual forb growing 10-30 cm in height with blue flowers, featuring an annual herb habit from a taproot, erect or nearly so stems 15-45 cm tall that are branched mostly at the base and sparsely appressed-hairy, and alternate, palmately compound leaves with 5 to 7 linear-oblong to narrowly elliptic leaflets measuring 1-4 cm long, having an upper surface that is smooth and hairless and green. Meadows and rocky areas.

Botanical Description

One of our small annual lupines with distinctive blue flowers. This species requires considerable drainage and can be found in gravel and shallow-soil meadows in full sun. A small annual forb growing 10-30 cm in height with blue flowers, featuring an annual herb habit from a taproot, erect or nearly so stems 15-45 cm tall that are branched mostly at the base and sparsely appressed-hairy, and alternate, palmately compound leaves with 5 to 7 linear-oblong to narrowly elliptic leaflets measuring 1-4 cm long, having an upper surface that is glabrous and green.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Meadows and rocky areas. Inhabiting dry sandy or gravelly, grassy meadows and clearings in the lowland zone; locally frequent on southeastern Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands, rare in Greater Vancouver, ranging south to California.

Habitat Types

Garden Uses

Meadow; Rock Garden; Hedgerow / Thicket

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Nitrogen Fixer

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Spread Behaviour

Deep-rooted non-spreading clump

Root Formation

Taproot

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM) & Rhizobial Nitrogen-Fixing (Bradyrhizobium / Rhizobium nodules)

Competition / Allelopathy

Symbiotic nitrogen-fixer that elevates site fertility and accelerates canopy closure; in low-nutrient Garry oak or alpine meadows, space carefully so vigorous nitrogen responders do not crowd slow forbs.

Pets, Children & Livestock

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Seeds and foliage contain quinolizidine alkaloids potentially toxic to livestock and pets if ingested raw in quantity.
