Overview

Summary

An annual forb growing 20-80 cm in height with yellow flowers, part of the suite of annual plants inhabiting mossy rocks, capable of long blooming in gardens, with edible roasted seeds. Found in meadows and rocky slopes, grassy tarweed inhabits dry roadsides, open slopes, and forest openings within lowland, steppe, and montane zones.

Botanical Description

Part of the suite of annual plants that make their living on mossy rocks. An annual forb growing 20-80 cm in height with yellow flowers, part of the suite of annual plants inhabiting mossy rocks.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Found in meadows and rocky slopes, grassy tarweed inhabits dry roadsides, open slopes, and forest openings within lowland, steppe, and montane zones. It is infrequent in extreme southern British Columbia, ranging south to Utah, Nevada, California, and Mexico, with a disjunct distribution in Chile. Grassy tarweed grows on open or partially shaded slopes or in flats in grasslands, meadows, shrublands, woodlands, forests, disturbed sites, stream banks, and roadsides (Jepson Flora Project, 2012). Grassy tarweed is native to western North America from southern British Columbia to northern Baja California and east to Utah and Montana, at elevations from sea level to 2377 m (Cronquist, 1961).

Soil Preferences

It often grows in coarse, sandy or gravelly soils, but is also found on silty/clayey soils, and sometimes on serpentine soils. It prefers full sun, but can tolerate light shade (Cronquist, 1961).

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Spread Behaviour

Deep-rooted non-spreading clump

Root Formation

Taproot

Mycorrhizal Association

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic and non-aggressive (Taproot); low root competition makes it safe for intimate mixed borders and diverse polyculture interplanting.

Erosion & Slope Control

Restoration: This native plant is useful to help control erosion in areas with disturbed soils that are low in nutrients.

Pruning & Management

Control of weedy perennial species that may out-compete grassy tarweed over time may be necessary. Encroachment of trees and shrubs will shade out this species. Although this species is an annual herb, it readily self-sows and persists.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

People of the Mendocino, Miwok, and Pomo tribes used seeds of grassy tarweed to make pinole, which was a staple food source. Food (roasted seeds). Edible seeds (roasted).

Edible Parts & Flavour

Safety

Edibility

Pets, Children & Livestock

Generally non-toxic; harvest away from roadsides and pesticides, and confirm species ID before consumption.

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

There is no current information on pests or diseases of this species. This plant readily self-sows and may spread and become weedy in pastures/rangeland or other neighboring habitat. It is avoided by livestock due to its odor, but is not known to be toxic. | Recorded insect/host associations (beees sheet): Astala polingi: on; Heliothis phloxiphaga: on leaves/stems

Propagation & Seeds

Sowing Protocol

Seeds germinate in cool temperatures and naturally germinate in late fall. Seeds should be broadcast on the site in the fall so they can germinate and become established over the winter. There are approximately 300,000 seeds per pound. Seeds can also be sown directly into holes in the weed fabric in late fall.

Propagation Methods

Contact your local Natural Resource Conservation Service office for more information on availability. Field establishment: For best establishment in seed production fields, grow plugs in a greenhouse (21°C days, 10°C nights) in late winter and transplant out in spring. Transplant into a field covered with weed fabric containing holes on 0.3 m by 0.3 m spacing.
