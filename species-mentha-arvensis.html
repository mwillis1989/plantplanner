Wild Mint

Mentha arvensis · Lamiaceae

Overview

Summary

Rhizomatous Perennial Forb, 30-80 cm height. Erect aromatic perennial herb, 15 to 50 cm high, finely hairy square stems; opposite short-petioled serrate leaves; small pink to pale purple/white flowers in dense axillary clusters; 4 brown ovoid nutlets.

Botanical Description

Aromatic perennial herb with square stems and strong menthol aroma. Aromatic, erect perennial herb, with pubescent square stems, 15 to 50 cm high; leaves short-petioled, opposite oblong-lanceolate to ovate lanceolate, 1 to 8 cm long, serrate, rounded at base; pink to pale purple or white small flowers in dense axillary clusters, corolla 4 to 6 mm long fused into 4 to 5-lobed tube; fruit four small, ovate nutlets at base of calyx, brown, ovoid, triangular at base, basal crescent-shaped depression. Rhizomatous Perennial Forb, 30-80 cm height.

Name Etymology

Genus Mentha; geographic specific epithet arvensis denotes the plant's North American regional distribution.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Edible

Soil Preferences

Moist to wet, rich loamy soils. Tolerates periods of flooding; adapted to medium and fine textured soils; can tolerate a pH ranging from 5 to 7.

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Spread Behaviour

Slowly spreading rhizomatous clump

Root Formation

Rhizomatous

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota endomycorrhizal network)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic moderate clonal spreader (Rhizomatous); weaves steadily through mixed groundcover matrices without harsh chemical inhibition.

Erosion & Slope Control

Cultivars not suitable for reclamation; industrial crop for menthol production.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Leaves boiled for mint tea/flavoring. Drunk as tea during 1918 flu pandemic. Mint tea made by boiling leaves.

Material & Technology

Industrial crop for menthol production in cosmetic, pharmaceutical, food, and flavouring industries.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Tea taken to treat cough, cold, congestion, fever, chills, menstrual cramps, soothe teething babies' gums, treat tiredness or fatigue, aid digestion, treat children's diarrhoea, treat headaches, high blood pressure, part of compound medicine for cancer, diabetes, pain, wash for sores. Ground flowers mixed with yarrow for gum infections/toothaches. Aromatic leaves and tops used as tea and inhalant for colds, fever, pains, swellings, colic in children, and summer complaint (headache, nosebleeds, sunstroke).
