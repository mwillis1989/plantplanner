Overview

Summary

Stoloniferous succulent perennial forb, 10-25 cm tall, featuring succulent leaves, trailing pinkish-white flowers, and performing well in dry shade, particularly on mossy rocks. Found on rock outcrops in meadows and woodlands, as well as moist mossy rock outcrops, streambanks, and shorelines in lowland and montane zones; frequent in southern British Columbia, ranging north to southeastern Alaska and southwestern Yukon, east to Manitoba, and south to Montana, Idaho, Utah, and California.

Botanical Description

Stoloniferous succulent perennial forb, 10-25 cm tall, featuring succulent leaves, trailing pinkish-white flowers, and performing well in dry shade, particularly on mossy rocks. Performs well in dry shade, especially on mossy rocks. Perennial herb arising from slender rhizomes and stolons, occasionally forming extensive patches; stems ascending to erect, solitary to several, simple from rhizome nodes, 10-25 cm tall; vegetative stoloniferous branches up to 40 cm long. Basal leaves numerous, egg-shaped, spoon-shaped, or rounded, with blades 1.5-3 cm long, 2-20 mm wide, and petioles equal to or exceeding the blades in length.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow; Woodland / Shade; Rock Garden; Rain Garden / Wetland; Coastal / Bluff

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Edible

Soil Preferences

Mossy rock outcrops, cliffs, and moist shaded stream banks. Dry to mesic or seasonally moist; thrives on mossy rocks.

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Spread Behaviour

Slowly spreading rhizomatous clump

Root Formation

Rhizomatous

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota endomycorrhizal network)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic moderate clonal spreader (Rhizomatous); weaves steadily through mixed groundcover matrices without harsh chemical inhibition.

Fall & Winter Interest

Year-round evergreen foliage anchors winter garden structure.
