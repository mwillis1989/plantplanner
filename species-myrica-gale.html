Sweet Gale

Myrica gale · Myricaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowShrubZone 4a–8b🦋 83 Lepidoptera hosts

Overview

Summary

Inhabiting swamps, bogs, fens, lakeshores, and estuary edges across lowland and montane zones, this circumboreal taxon is common in coastal British Columbia, rare in south-central and northeastern British Columbia, ranging north to Alaska, Yukon, and Northwest Territories, east to Newfoundland, and south to Minnesota, Tennessee, North Carolina, and Oregon, as well as Eurasia.

Botanical Description

Shrub 0.5-2 m tall, spreading by suckers, featuring spreading stems that are finely hairy when young and glabrous at maturity, loosely branched with dark reddish bark; leaves alternate, deciduous, oblanceolate to more oblong, 3-6 cm long, up to 2 cm wide, entire to coarsely few-toothed above the middle, hairy on both surfaces or rarely glabrous below, strongly dotted with yellow wax-glands, rounded to blunt at the apex, and aromatic. Fragrance leaves. Deciduous aromatic shrub with fragrant, resinous leaves.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Rain Garden / Wetland; Coastal / Bluff; Hedgerow / Thicket

Key Features

Butterfly / Moth Host; Nitrogen Fixer; Edible

Soil Preferences

Wet, acidic, peat or muddy soils.

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Spread Behaviour

Slowly spreading rhizomatous clump

Root Formation

Rhizomatous

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM) & Actinorhizal Nitrogen-Fixing (Frankia root nodules)

Competition / Allelopathy

Symbiotic nitrogen-fixer that elevates site fertility and accelerates canopy closure; in low-nutrient Garry oak or alpine meadows, space carefully so vigorous nitrogen responders do not crowd slow forbs.
