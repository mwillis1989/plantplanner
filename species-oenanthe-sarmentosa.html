Pacific Water-parsley

Oenanthe sarmentosa · Apiaceae

Overview

Summary

Stoloniferous Perennial Forb up to 40 cm height with divided foliage and white umbel flowers. Inhabits wetlands, wet streamsides, marshes, meadows, and open forests in the lowland and montane zones; common in and west of the Coast-Cascade Mountains, ranging north to Alaska and south to California, and found in wetland habitats along streams in Zone 3 alder stands of Littlewood Forest.

Botanical Description

Distinctive divided foliage and umbels of white flowers are attractive in full sun to full shade wet spots in rich soils. Roots of plant can be below water year-round. A stoloniferous, fibrous-rooted perennial forb up to 40 cm (or up to 1 m) in height, featuring soft, weak, loosely branched stems that are reclining to ascending, sometimes rooting at the nodes, and glabrous. Foliage is basal and stem-borne, pinnately divided 2-3 times with toothed and cleft leaflets, where primary lateral veins are directed to the marginal teeth.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Stems eaten.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Medicinal laxative, vomiting remedy. Prepared as a potent traditional laxative. Traditionally utilized cautiously as a laxative or lure medicine.
