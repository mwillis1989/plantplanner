Satinflower

Olsynium douglasii · Iridaceae

Overview

Summary

Perennial Forb, 10-30 cm height with purple/blueish-purple flowers. Inhabits rocky slopes and meadows, specifically vernally moist rocky bluffs, grassy slopes, and Quercus garryana woodlands in the lowland zone (var. douglasii) and sagebrush slopes in the montane zone (var. inflatum); var. douglasii is infrequent on southern Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands and ranges south to northern California, while var. inflatum is rare in south-central British Columbia and ranges south to Utah, Nevada, and Oregon.

Botanical Description

Delicate plants bloom early in the spring, sometimes by late February, ushering in a new season. A delicate perennial tufted herb from a fibrous root, 10-30 cm in height, which thrives in dry, rocky habitats in full sun and blooms early in spring (sometimes by late February) to usher in a new season with purple to bluish-purple flowers. Stems somewhat compressed, simple, 10-30 cm tall. Basal leaves are reduced and bractlike, with blades lacking or occasionally 1-2 cm long, while stem leaves number 2 to 4, are linear, positioned on the lower 1/2 of the stem, sheathing, with blades 10-30 cm long, 1.5-3 mm wide, and long-pointed tips.

Name Etymology

Genus Olsynium; specific epithet douglasii honors a botanical collector or naturalist associated with Satinflower.
