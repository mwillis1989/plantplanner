Brittle Prickly-Pear

Overview

Summary

A low, mat-forming succulent perennial herb from a fibrous root, growing up to 15 cm tall with prostrate, fleshy, subglobose to rounded stem segments 2-5 cm long that readily detach; areoles are coarsely white-woolly with a few yellowish, spiny bristles and 2-7 strongly barbed, straight, yellowish to brownish spines 1-3 cm long, and bear showy, tissue paper-like yellow flowers. Found in dry open sites, sandy soil, gravelly soil, and rock outcrops, specifically on dry grassy, sandy, or gravelly slopes in the lowland, steppe, and montane zones; common in southern British Columbia east of the Coast-Cascade Mountains, less frequent on southeastern Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands, and rare along the Peace River in northeastern British Columbia, extending east to Ontario and south to Kansas, Illinois, Texas, and northern California.

Botanical Description

Perennial herb from a fibrous root; mat-forming; stems prostrate, succulent, subglobose to rounded, fleshy, 5-20 cm tall; stem segments not much flattened, readily detaching from the plant, round to egg-shaped, 2-5 cm long; areoles coarsely white-woolly with a few yellowish, spiny bristles and 2-7 straight, yellowish to brownish, 1-3 cm long spines. Has large spines with smaller yellowish bristles. Tissue paper-like flowers are yellow, showy, and large. Happy on either side of the Cascades.

Name Etymology

Genus Opuntia; species Opuntia fragilis.
