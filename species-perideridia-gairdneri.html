Yampah

Perideridia gairdneri · Apiaceae

Overview

Summary

Perennial Forb, 30-90 cm height with slender hairless stems, thumb-sized brown almond-like tuberous root, linear divided leaves, and small white umbel inflorescences. Found in meadows, woodlands, and coastal bluffs, including moist to dry meadows and woodlands in lowland and montane zones; infrequent in southeastern and southwestern British Columbia (known from southeastern Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands), also rare in south-central British Columbia; ranging east to Saskatchewan and south to South Dakota, Colorado, New Mexico, and California.

Botanical Description

Delicate perennial forb (40-90 cm) growing from a tuberous-thickened edible root with white terminal umbels. Solitary stems give rise to finely dissected leaves and white, terminal umbel inflorescences. The foliage is highly variable. This is one of southern Vancouver Islands latest flowering meadow plants that grows in meadows and open woodlands.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Found in meadows, woodlands, and coastal bluffs, including moist to dry meadows and woodlands in lowland and montane zones; infrequent in southeastern and southwestern British Columbia (known from southeastern Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands), also rare in south-central British Columbia; ranging east to Saskatchewan and south to South Dakota, Colorado, New Mexico, and California. Also occurs in moist edges of open hillsides, forests, and scab rock flats from sea level to 2286 m, and in cultivated gardens in the San Juan and Gulf Islands.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow; Woodland / Shade; Rock Garden; Hedgerow / Thicket

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Edible

Soil Preferences

Moist meadows, open hillsides, Garry oak savannas, mossy slopes, and stream edges (sea level to 2,300 m). Dry to mesic. Deep anthropogenic garden soils.

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Spread Behaviour

Slowly multiplying clump from underground bulbs or corms

Root Formation

Bulb / Corm / Tuber

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota endomycorrhizal network)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic and non-aggressive (Bulb / Corm / Tuber); low root competition makes it safe for intimate mixed borders and diverse polyculture interplanting.

Erosion & Slope Control

Grown and harvested in multi-species gardens.

Pruning & Management

Cultivated by leaving/knocking seeds into turned earth, cleaning rootlets on-site, and weeding competing plants.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Food (raw or cooked roots). Food (edible roots / "wild carrot" chewed to suppress hunger). Nutty roots harvested in late spring, eaten boiled, raw, steamed, roasted, dried, made into mush or pinole, or used as flour/flavoring (Straits Salish, Klamath, Modoc, Blackfoot, Paiute, Cheyenne).

Edible Parts & Flavour

Edible nutty roots (raw, boiled, roasted, steamed, dried, or ground into pinole/flour).

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Root preparations used medicinally for digestive strength and general illness.

Safety

Pets, Children & Livestock

Propagation & Seeds

Sowing Protocol

Sow in fall for spring germination. Cold stratification required.

Propagation Methods

Cultivated during harvest by weeding beds, loosening soil, and scattering ripe seeds back into harvest holes. Benefitted by burning.
