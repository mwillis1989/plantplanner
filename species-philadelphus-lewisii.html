Overview

Summary

Fast-growing, adaptable, broad upright Deciduous Shrub up to 3-4 m height with large white early fragrant flowers. Found in thickets, woodlands, moist to dry talus slopes, shrublands, streamsides, and open forests across lowland, steppe, and montane zones; frequent in southern British Columbia; extending east to southwestern Alberta and south to Montana, Idaho, and northern California.

Botanical Description

A showy fast-growing fragrant shrub with large white flowers that are sure to attract Western Tiger Swallowtail, Pale Swallowtail, Anna's Hummingbird, and a plethora of native bees. A fast-growing, adaptable, broad upright deciduous shrub, erect and loosely branched, reaching 1.5-3 m tall (or 3-4 m height, and 0.9–3 m tall). Deciduous shrub, erect, loosely branched, 1.5-3 m tall; bark reddish-brown, turning grey and flaking off in patches; twigs glabrous to hairy.

Name Etymology

Genus Philadelphus; specific epithet lewisii honors a botanical collector or naturalist associated with Mock Orange.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Found in thickets, woodlands, moist to dry talus slopes, shrublands, streamsides, and open forests across lowland, steppe, and montane zones; frequent in southern British Columbia; extending east to southwestern Alberta and south to Montana, Idaho, and northern California. Thickets and woodlands; dry, well-drained soils. Lewis' mockorange occurs on well-drained, moist sites, growing on deep, rich alluvial loams to rocky or gravelly loams. It is commonly found on rocky sites, at the base of talus slopes and cliffs, along streams, and in seasonally moist draws, and is found at talus margins in the Columbia River Basin.

Propagation & Seeds

Sowing Protocol

Three samples of seed averaged 5,300,000 seeds per pound. Fall sowing is an alternative to cold stratification.

Propagation Methods

The Pullman Plant Materials Center, NRCS, Pullman, Washington released two mockoranges in 2002 as selected class releases. These were St. Maries Germplasm (St. Maries, Idaho) and Colfax Germplasm (Colfax, Washington). Lewis' mockorange is propagated via seeds, hardwood and softwood cuttings. Vegetative propagation of cultivar material is recommended to maintain uniformity of desired characteristics. Lewis mockorange is readily propagated by seed, stem cuttings, layering, root suckers, or division.
