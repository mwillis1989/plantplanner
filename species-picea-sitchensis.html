Sitka Spruce

Picea sitchensis · Pinaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowTreeZone 4a–8b🦋 91 Lepidoptera hosts

Overview

Summary

Large coniferous tree with sharp needles; light and exceptionally strong wood. Inhabiting moist to mesic slopes and river terraces in lowland and montane zones; common in extreme western British Columbia; ranging north to southeastern Alaska and south to northern California.

Botanical Description

Large coniferous tree reaching up to 90 m tall (up to two hundred feet) with a crown of noticeably long, horizontal main branches and drooping branchlets. Bark is gray and smooth on small trunks, becoming dark grayish-brown to purplish and thin-scaly on older trunks; young twigs are glabrous. Needles are flattened, stiff, very sharp, yellowish-green to bluish-green, 2.5-3.8 cm (1 to 1½ inches) long, standing out in all directions, pungent, with white lines of stomata on the upper surface. Seed cones are 5-9 cm (one to four inches) long, hanging down, reddish-brown to yellowish-brown, with very thin, rounded scales featuring finely irregularly toothed tips.

Name Etymology

Genus Picea; geographic specific epithet sitchensis denotes the plant's North American regional distribution.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Inhabiting moist to mesic slopes and river terraces in lowland and montane zones; common in extreme western British Columbia; ranging north to southeastern Alaska and south to northern California. Flourishing in moist, well-drained coastal forests, Sitka spruce is native to the Pacific Coast region from Alaska to western British Columbia, Washington, Oregon, and northwestern California.

Habitat Types

Forests; Riparian & Streambanks; Wetlands & Marshes; Marine Shorelines

Biogeoclimatic Zones

BC Regional Range

West of Cascade Crest; Vancouver Island; Gulf Islands; Lower Mainland

Elevation

Successional Role

Successional stages of Thuja-Alnus forests. Alnus-Athyrium forest with Thuja plicata, Tsuga heterophylla and Picea sitchensis.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Rock Garden; Rain Garden / Wetland; Coastal / Bluff

Key Features

Soil Preferences

Picea sitchensis is often found on moist well-drained sites such as alluvial floodplains, marine terraces, and headlands MacKinnon 1994.

Moisture Notes

Thrives in seasonally saturated soils, riparian benches, or poorly drained depressions.

Spread Behaviour

Woody crown and lateral root system

Root Formation

Deep lateral / Taproot / Woody burl

Mycorrhizal Association

Ectomycorrhizal (EcM; Suillus, Russula, Lactarius, Amanita & forest truffle symbionts)

Competition / Allelopathy

Acidic needle duff, dense feeder-root competition, and year-round canopy shade out sun-loving meadow herbs; underplant with adapted acidophilic woodland understory flora.

Fall & Winter Interest

Year-round evergreen foliage anchors winter garden structure.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

New leaves useful as tea. The inner bark was eaten fresh, or dried into cakes and eaten with berries. It was also dried and ground into a powder and used as a thickener in soups or added to cereals when making bread.

Edible Parts & Flavour

Wood used where extreme strength-to-weight ratio is required.

Material & Technology

Branches and roots processed for cordage. Roots used in basketry. Strong light wood utilized for construction and crafting.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Sitka spruce was widely employed medicinally by several native North American Indian tribes who used it especially for its antiseptic qualities in the treatment of lung complaints, sores and wounds. It has been chewed in the treatment of throat problems, coughs, and colds. Sitka spruce pitch was also used as a medicine for gonorrhea, syphilis, internal swelling, and toothaches.

Propagation & Seeds

Stratification & Dormancy

Picea sitchensis seed requires no pretreatment if the seed is sown fresh, however a period of cold unifies and hastens germination (Dirr & Heuser 1987). Sow stored seeds as early in the year as possible.

Sowing Protocol

Preferably sow the seeds in a position in light shade.

Propagation Methods

Picea sitchensis seed requires no pretreatment if the seed is sown fresh, however a period of cold unifies and hastens germination.
