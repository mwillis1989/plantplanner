Shore Pine

Pinus contorta · Pinaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowTreeZone 1a–8b🦋 130 Lepidoptera hosts
🔎 Open 5-Tab Explorer Profile📊 Compare in Native Plant MatrixE-Flora BC ↗

Overview

Summary

Inhabits wet to dry bogs, lower slopes, and high river terraces across lowland, montane, and subalpine zones. Common throughout British Columbia; ranging north to south in Alaska, Yukon Territory, and southwestern Northwest Territories, east to west in Alberta, and south to California, Utah, Colorado, and South Dakota.

Botanical Description

Tree, mostly to 30 m tall, but up to 40 m; var. latifolia mostly straight and columnar with narrow rounded crown, var. contorta often somewhat contorted and even bonsai-like; bark reddish-brown to blackish, cracked at the base and somewhat flaky above, furrowed and thicker in var. contorta. Var. latifolia is mostly straight and columnar with a narrow rounded crown and yellowish-green needles in groups of two, usually curved and slightly twisted, 2-7 cm long, with seed cones pointing outward. Var. contorta is often somewhat contorted and even bonsai-like, with deep green needles, seed cones pointing backwards on the twig, furrowed and thicker bark, and reddish-brown to blackish bark that is cracked at the base and somewhat flaky above. Seed cones are egg-shaped, lopsided, 3-6 cm long, serotinous, with stiff bracts and armed tips.

Common throughout British Columbia; ranging north to south in Alaska, Yukon Territory, and southwestern Northwest Territories, east to west in Alberta, and south to California, Utah, Colorado, and South Dakota.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Woodland / Shade; Rain Garden / Wetland

Key Features

Butterfly / Moth Host; Winter Interest; Edible

Soil Preferences

Muck and peat, strong accumulations of organic soil material. Peat, 2 to several feet thick over clay. Dry interior forests, sandy coastal dunes, and peat bogs.

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Spread Behaviour

Woody crown and lateral root system

Root Formation

Deep lateral / Taproot / Woody burl

Mycorrhizal Association

Ectomycorrhizal (EcM; Suillus, Russula, Lactarius, Amanita & forest truffle symbionts)

Competition / Allelopathy

Acidic needle duff, dense feeder-root competition, and year-round canopy shade out sun-loving meadow herbs; underplant with adapted acidophilic woodland understory flora.

Fall & Winter Interest

Year-round evergreen foliage anchors winter garden structure.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Seeds edible but very small. New needles used for tea. Food (edible inner bark/cambium/sap), pitch collected as chewing gum, tattoo material.

Material & Technology

Edible inner bark (cambium), pitch for tattooing, poles for lodge construction.

Safety

Edibility

Yes

Pets, Children & Livestock

Generally non-toxic; harvest away from roadsides and pesticides, and confirm species ID before consumption.
