Sea Blush

Plectritis congesta · Caprifoliaceae

Overview

Summary

An annual forb / wildflower, 5-60 cm in height with dainty pink tufted spring flowers and dimorphic seeds; annual herb from a taproot; stems erect, solitary, simple, smooth, 10-60 cm tall; basal leaves soon deciduous; stem leaves opposite, the lowermost leaves spoon-shaped or egg-shaped with short stalks, others more oblong or elliptic and stalkless, smooth, 1-6 cm long, 3-22 mm wide. Found in meadows, rocky slopes, and woodlands, as well as mesic to vernally moist meadows or dry rocky sites in the lowland zone; common in southwestern British Columbia, and rare in the Queen Charlotte Islands; ranging south to California, and occurring in meadows, rocky slopes, woodlands, ocean shores, coastal bluffs, and seasonally moist sites.

Botanical Description

This native annual is best sown as seed in fall to form distinctive green carpets of small leaves through winter and flowering pink in spring. Look for their dimorphic seeds (two-shaped), with some looking like small flax seeds and the others as husks. Sea Blush is tolerant of a variety of moisture and light regimes, from dry through vernally wet and full sun to part shade, and is often grown with Shortspur Sea Blush.

Propagation & Seeds

Stratification & Dormancy

Establishment is easiest by directly seeding into the site in the fall or early spring.

Sowing Protocol

Seed lacks dormancy so does not require pre - treatment, and can be broadcast on the soil surface at a rate of 2.2 kg/ha and pressed in with a roller or harrow.

Propagation Methods

Sow seeds in fall or early spring; self-sows. There are no improved materials, but seed of local ecotypes is sometimes available from commercial sources.
