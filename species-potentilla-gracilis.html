Graceful Cinquefoil

Potentilla gracilis Douglas ex Hook. · Rosaceae

Overview

Summary

Perennial Forb, 30-80 cm height with lemon-yellow flowers. Found in dry to moist meadows, grasslands, rocky slopes, open forests, roadsides, and waste places from the lowland to montane zones; common in southern British Columbia except rare on the coast (var. gracilis), and rare in British Columbia north of 55°N; ranging north to Alaska, east to Saskatchewan, and south to California and Mexico.

Botanical Description

Early summer lemon-yellow blooms and distinctive foliage. Plant in a full sun area. Soils can be dry, but it also is very tolerant of wet winter meadows that dry in summer. A perennial forb and native wildflower of the rose family (Rosaceae), also known as slender cinquefoil, characterized by early summer lemon-yellow blooms, a height of 30-80 cm (stems 20.3–101.6 cm), and a thick, scaly, branched stem-base/rhizome.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Found in dry to moist meadows, grasslands, rocky slopes, open forests, roadsides, and waste places from the lowland to montane zones; common in southern British Columbia except rare on the coast (var. gracilis), and rare in British Columbia north of 55°N; ranging north to Alaska, east to Saskatchewan, and south to California and Mexico. Slender cinquefoil is frequently encountered in meadows, grasslands, roadsides, subalpine meadows, open forests, and moist areas within deserts and arid shrub-steppe regions at elevations up to 3505 m, and is widely distributed throughout North America from Alaska to Baja California, extending east through the northern Great Plains to the Great Lakes Basin and Quebec.

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic but vigorous clonal spreader (Rhizomatous); forms thickets that outcompete slow-establishing forbs—allow generous spacing or use root barriers in tight borders.

Erosion & Slope Control

Fibrous root system stabilizes disturbed meadow and slope soils and helps resist invasion by non-native species.

Pruning & Management

Slender cinquefoil requires little maintenance once established and will generally persist for 3 to 10 years. For seed production fields, seeds are usually started as large plugs in the greenhouse during the winter and planted out in the spring on 30.5 cm within row spacing and 18-to 61 cm between row spacing. Transplants will flower and produce seed one year after transplanting. Large seed production fields can be direct combined, while plants in small fields are generally swathed and collected to dry under cover on tarps or in bags.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Slender cinquefoil was used by tribes of the Pacific Northwest for various medicinal purposes. The Syilx (Okanagan) / Colville tribe made an infusion of pounded roots as a tonic for the blood, general aches and pains, diarrhea, gonorrhea, and for washing sores. The Nlaka'pamux (Thompson) tribe used a poultice of mashed leaves and roots on wounds to draw out the pain.

Safety

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

This species has little potential to become weedy or invasive. There is, however, a similar looking, related Eurasian species, sulfur cinquefoil (Potentilla recta), that is considered a noxious weed in Colorado, Montana, Nevada, Oregon, Washington, and British Colombia. | Recorded insect/host associations (beees sheet): Eupithecia nimbicolor: on flowers, on leaves; Ancylis comptana: on; Aphelia alleniana: in fruits/leaves; Cheimophila salicella: on; Gynaephora groenlandica: on flowers; Sparganothis unifasciana: on leaves; Synanthedon bibionipennis: in roots/stems

Propagation & Seeds

Seed Collection

Seeds are typically mature by late summer early fall. Transplants will flower and produce seed one year after transplanting.

Stratification & Dormancy

Seeds exhibit physiological dormancy which requires 60 to 120 days cold-moist stratification or fall sowing to break dormancy, depending on the latitude and elevation of the source population.

Sowing Protocol

Seeds should be lightly covered, and usually germinate within one to two weeks after appropriate stratification. Cinquefoil may easily be established as a transplant from container stock in the fall or spring. For seed production fields, seeds are usually started as large plugs in the greenhouse during the winter and planted out in the spring on 30.5 cm within row spacing and 18-to 61 cm between row spacing.

Propagation Methods

There are no developed cultivars of slender cinquefoil, but both non-certified and certified source identified seed is widely available from commercial sources. Container plants are also sometimes available from native plant nurseries.
