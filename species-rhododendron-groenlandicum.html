Labrador Tea

Rhododendron groenlandicum · Ericaceae

Overview

Summary

Perennial, erect, aromatic shrub, 30 to 80 cm high; rhizomatous; alternate oblong to elliptic evergreen leaves, densely rusty-tomentose beneath; terminal umbel of white flowers; oval/oblong capsule fruit. Occurring in bogs and moist to wet forests in the lowland and montane zones; common throughout most of British Columbia, infrequent in south-central British Columbia; north to Alaska, east to Newfoundland, and south to Oregon, Minnesota, Pennsylvania, and New Jersey.

Botanical Description

Erect shrub, 0.5-1.5 m tall, with rusty soft-hairy stems. Leaves are alternate, evergreen, leathery, oblong to lance-oblong or elliptic, (1.2) 2-6 cm long, 3-15 mm wide, with margins strongly to slightly rolled under, blunt tips, upper surface deep green, appearing somewhat wrinkled, glabrous to slightly reddish soft-hairy, and copiously rusty or yellowish soft-hairy beneath; stalks are 1-5 mm long. Fragrant leaves. Low evergreen aromatic shrub (30–80 cm tall) with linear-elliptic leaves featuring revolute margins and dense rusty wool (tomentum) underneath.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Occurring in bogs and moist to wet forests in the lowland and montane zones; common throughout most of British Columbia, infrequent in south-central British Columbia; north to Alaska, east to Newfoundland, and south to Oregon, Minnesota, Pennsylvania, and New Jersey. Associated with acidic and moist organic substrates including bogs, muskegs, swamps, and wet coniferous woods across a pH range of 2.9 to 6.9, spanning the boreal forest and tundra from Alaska to Newfoundland and south to Oregon, Pennsylvania, and New Jersey. Wetlands. Bog Labrador tea grows in bogs, fens, muskegs, open tundra, and dwarf shrub communities.

Successional Role

Post-fire peatland shrub. Peatland / bog climax shrub.

Fire Ecology

Indians in different regions burned these wetlands periodically (Anderson 2009; Patterson 2003). The primary role of fire was to maintain open habitats such as prairies, bogs, and forest clearings.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Woodland / Shade; Rain Garden / Wetland; Hedgerow / Thicket

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Winter Interest; Edible

Soil Preferences

Acidic, moist organic substrates: bogs, muskegs, peat swamps, and wet coniferous woods (pH 2.9–6.9). Wet, acidic peat soils.

Moisture Notes

Thrives in seasonally saturated soils, riparian benches, or poorly drained depressions.

Spread Behaviour

Slowly spreading rhizomatous clump

Root Formation

Rhizomatous

Mycorrhizal Association

Ericoid Mycorrhiza (ErM; Rhizoscyphus ericae & acidophilic humus fungi)

Competition / Allelopathy

Forms tight ericaceous root mats and phenolic leaf litter that gradually acidifies surface humus and slows annual weed invasion; compatible with acid-tolerant woodland companions.

Erosion & Slope Control

Naturally re-colonizes sites disturbed by logging, burning, mining, roads; re-establishes rapidly after fire; useful in revegetation; indicator for lead contamination.

Fall & Winter Interest

Persistent fruit, hips, cones, or dried seedheads add late-season visual interest and winter wildlife forage.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Leaves used as tea (caution: causes dizziness/drowsiness in excess). Substitute for bay leaf in stews, sauces, and soups. Valued by Coast Salish peoples for brewing tea.

Edible Parts & Flavour

Leaves used as tea (caution: causes dizziness/drowsiness in excess); bay leaf substitute in stews; berries stewed with smoked fish.

Material & Technology

Berries provide red dye.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Leaves chewed or made into tea to treat stomach flu, chills, pneumonia, headaches, diarrhoea, heart ailments, arthritis, whooping cough, teething pain, kidney ailments, and relieve tension. Powdered leaves applied to burns and eczema. Leaf decoctions used as an eye-wash for dry eyes. The Makah, for example used a strong infusion for a blood purifier.

Safety

Edibility

Yes

Pets, Children & Livestock

Generally non-toxic; harvest away from roadsides and pesticides, and confirm species ID before consumption.

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Recorded insect/host associations (beees sheet): Coleophora ledi: on leaves; Incisalia augustinus: on flowers/fruits/leaves

Propagation & Seeds

Stratification & Dormancy

Collect the seeds from dry capsules and plant them in fall or spring in a moist peaty soil, in a sunny spot.

Propagation Methods

Propagated by rhizome division, stem cuttings, or seeds (requires light and acidic peat substrate). It is available from native plant nurseries (Damrosch 2008). Plants can be started from seed or root - crown division.
