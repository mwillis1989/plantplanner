Overview

Summary

Compact deciduous shrub without thorns, up to 1-3 m height; crinkly maple-like leaves; clusters of brilliant hot pink to red early blooms; dark greyish fruits. Inhabits woodlands, forest edges, moist to dry forests, and rock outcrops across lowland and montane zones; frequent in southwestern and southeastern British Columbia north to 52°N, and south to California, as well as dry open areas on mountainsides at mid to low elevations.

Botanical Description

Deciduous shrub up to 2 m with clusters of light to hot-pink flowers. A compact deciduous shrub reaching 1-3 m in height, unarmed with brownish to reddish-brown bark, producing clusters of light- to hot-pink or brilliant hot pink to red early blooms and dark greyish fruits. Deciduous shrub, loosely branched, 1-3 m tall; stems erect, finely hairy, unarmed; bark brownish to reddish-brown. Leaves are alternate, broadly kidney-shaped or rounded, shallowly heart-shaped at the base, 2-7 cm wide, and equally to irregularly 5-lobed nearly half their length with rounded, shallowly 2-3 toothed lobes that are glabrous above and finely hairy below.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Inhabits woodlands, forest edges, moist to dry forests, and rock outcrops across lowland and montane zones; frequent in southwestern and southeastern British Columbia north to 52°N, and south to California, as well as dry open areas on mountainsides at mid to low elevations.

BC Regional Range

Vancouver Island; Gulf Islands; Lower Mainland; Coast Mountains; Southern Interior; West of Cascade Crest; East of Cascade Crest

Elevation

Lowland to Montane (0–1200 m)

Successional Role

Early successional pioneer following fire openings. Succession to the Pseudotsuga succession.

Fire Ecology

Early successional pioneer following fire openings. Succession to the Pseudotsuga "fire climax" succession. Red-flowering currant is a pioneer species sprouting from seed or root crowns following fire.

Food Uses

Edible fruit with enough sugar, edible blossoms. Berries eaten for food. Food (edible berries).

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Seed Collection

Heavy seed crops occur every second or third year and 100 lbs. of berries will yield about 4 lbs. of seed.
