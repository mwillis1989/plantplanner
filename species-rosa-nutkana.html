Nootka Rose

Overview

Summary

Rhizomatous Deciduous Shrub up to 0.5-3 m height with large open aromatic pink/lilac-pink flowers, thorny stems, and large reddish-orange rose hips high in Vit C. Inhabiting meadows, riparian areas, mesic to moist thickets, forest edges, river terraces, shorelines, streambanks, clearings, and roadsides in lowland and montane zones.

Botanical Description

Large open pink flowers that are aromatic and pollinator-friendly. Rosa nutkana is a rhizomatous deciduous shrub reaching 0.5 to 3 m in height (0.3–2.7 m tall), characterized by large, open, aromatic pink to lilac-pink flowers and stout to spindly, erect to arching stems that become blackish at maturity.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Inhabiting meadows, riparian areas, mesic to moist thickets, forest edges, river terraces, shorelines, streambanks, clearings, and roadsides in lowland and montane zones. Common throughout British Columbia south of 55°N (except in the Chilcotin area), infrequent northward, and absent in northeastern British Columbia; ranging north to Alaska and south to California, Colorado, and Utah. Rosa nutkana is an understory plant in dry and moist forest communities, including Douglas-fir (Pseudotsuga menziesii), ponderosa pine (Pinus ponderosa), mountain hemlock (Tsuga mertensiana), Sitka spruce (Picea sitchensis), lodgepole pine (Pinus contorta), redwood (Sequoia sempervirens) and western hardwoods (Reed, 1993). It is often found in association with red-osier dogwood (Cornus sericea), quaking aspen (Populus tremuloides), black cottonwood (Populus trichocarpa) and snowberry (Symphoricarpos albus) (Aller et al., 1981).

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow; Woodland / Shade; Rock Garden; Rain Garden / Wetland; Coastal / Bluff

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Bird Forage; Erosion Control; Winter Interest; Edible

Soil Preferences

It is adapted to medium and fine textured soils with neutral pH (USDA NRCS, 2012). It can tolerate low levels of fertility but does not tolerate drought (USDA NRCS, 2012).

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Spread Behaviour

Rhizomatous colony- or thicket-former

Root Formation

Rhizomatous

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota endomycorrhizal network)

Competition / Allelopathy

Strong clonal colony competitor (Rhizomatous); moderate allelopathic/litter suppression combined with dense rhizome or sucker expansion—give clear spacing from delicate spring ephemerals.

Erosion & Slope Control

Stabilizes stream banks.

Fall & Winter Interest

Persistent fruit, hips, cones, or dried seedheads add late-season visual interest and winter wildlife forage.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Hips of all wild roses are high in vitamin C and are made into jams, jellies, syrups and teas. Food (fruit & petals intended for salad and tea). Flower petals, young spring shoots (the’thqi), and ripe rosehips eaten fresh, cooked into jams, or brewed for tea (high in vitamin C).

Edible Parts & Flavour

Fruit & Petals (Salad and Tea Intended); Edible fruit/hips, petals, and sweet young shoots (the’thqi) for salad, tea, and jelly.

Material & Technology

Roots used in reef net construction.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Fruit/hips, petals, and young shoots used medicinally. Rosehips, petals, young leaves, and peeled shoots utilized as medicine. Rosehip fruit harvested when ripe for medicine.

Safety

Edibility

Yes

Pets, Children & Livestock

Generally non-toxic; harvest away from roadsides and pesticides, and confirm species ID before consumption.

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Armed with sharp prickles and thorns along the stems; otherwise non-toxic.

Propagation & Seeds

Seed Collection

Rosa nutkana plants are sexually reproductive after 2 to 5 years of growth. Seed is obtained by collecting rose hips after they turn a bright red color. The seeds can be removed from the hip flesh by macerating the hips in water and allowing the debris to float to the surface.

Stratification & Dormancy

Dried seed needs a cold stratification period of 90 days for optimal germination.

Sowing Protocol

Freshly cleaned Nootka rose seed can be broadcast or drilled and covered with firm soil and mulch. Plants can also be established by transplanting seedlings or cuttings (see the Seeds and Plant Production section, below).
