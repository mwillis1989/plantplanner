Overview

Summary

A deciduous dioecious shrub or tree, 0.6-8 m tall, not colonial, producing beautifully large catkins (pussywillows) in early spring followed by soft, silvery leaves. Found along shorelines and in wetlands, including swamps, lakeshores, and moist interdunal depressions, this plant is common in coastal southwestern British Columbia and the Queen Charlotte Islands, ranging northward to southern Alaska and southward to northern California.

Botanical Description

A deciduous dioecious shrub or tree, 0.6-8 m tall, not colonial, producing beautifully large catkins (pussywillows) in early spring followed by soft, silvery leaves. Male and female flowers are on different plants (dioecious). On the east side of Vancouver Island, this functions as a wetland plant, whereas on the outer coast it frequently forms thickets just above the driftwood line. Dioecious shrubs or trees, 0.6-8 m tall, not colonial; branches erect, flexible at base; twigs yellow- to red-brown, smooth or densely hairy.
