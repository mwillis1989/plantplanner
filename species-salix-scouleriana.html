Scouler's Willow

Salix scouleriana Barratt · Salicaceae

Overview

Summary

Dioecious Deciduous Tree/tall spindly shrub up to 15 m height with velvety young leaves/twigs and large catkins. Inhabiting forests, thickets, and woodlands, specifically dry to moist open forests, thickets, clearings, and edges of rivers and lakes in the lowland, steppe, and montane zones; common throughout British Columbia, ranging north to Alaska, Yukon Territory, and Northwest Territories, east to Saskatchewan, and south to South Dakota, Arizona, New Mexico, and California.

Botanical Description

This native willow stands alone as being the most drought-tolerant willow of our region. Small deciduous tree up to 15 m with attractive foliage and large catkins. Males and female flowers are on different plants (dioecious). Shrubs or trees dioecious, 3-20 m tall, not colonial; branches erect, flexible at base; twigs yellow-green to yellow-brown, sparsely to densely velvety-hairy.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Inhabiting forests, thickets, and woodlands, specifically dry to moist open forests, thickets, clearings, and edges of rivers and lakes in the lowland, steppe, and montane zones; common throughout British Columbia, ranging north to Alaska, Yukon Territory, and Northwest Territories, east to Saskatchewan, and south to South Dakota, Arizona, New Mexico, and California. Also found in wetlands, stream banks, and clearings.

Habitat Types

Forests; Woodlands; Prairies; Riparian & Streambanks; Wetlands & Marshes; Marine Shorelines; Thickets & Clearings

Biogeoclimatic Zones

Coastal Douglas-fir (CDFmm); Coastal Western Hemlock (CWHxm); Bunchgrass (BG); Ponderosa Pine (PP); Interior Douglas-fir (IDF)

BC Regional Range

Vancouver Island; Gulf Islands; Lower Mainland; Coast Mountains; Southern Interior; West of Cascade Crest; East of Cascade Crest

Elevation

Lowland to Steppe to Montane (0–1200 m)

Successional Role

Succession to Pseudotsuga; abundant early in succession, declines after fire of light to medium intensity. Early to Mid-Successional (Inferred).

Fire Ecology

Succession to Pseudotsuga fire climax; abundant early in succession, declines after fire of light to medium intensity.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Meadow; Woodland / Shade; Rock Garden; Rain Garden / Wetland; Coastal / Bluff

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Bird Forage; Drought Tolerant

Soil Preferences

Mesic to moist; highly drought-tolerant for a willow. Moist, riparian, or wetland soils.

Moisture Notes

Adapted to well-drained winter-wet soils with prolonged summer dry periods.

Spread Behaviour

Woody crown and lateral root system

Root Formation

Deep lateral / Taproot / Woody burl

Mycorrhizal Association

Dual Ectomycorrhizal (EcM) & Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota)

Competition / Allelopathy

Non-allelopathic and non-aggressive (Deep lateral / Taproot / Woody burl); low root competition makes it safe for intimate mixed borders and diverse polyculture interplanting.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Edible Parts & Flavour

Bark chewed for aspirin-like pain/fever relief; branch tea for post-partum bleeding.

Material & Technology

Stems/branches woven into baskets. Bark stripped for clothing, bags, and rope fibers. Lightweight wood used for prosthetics.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Bark chewed for aspirin-like pain relief and fever reduction. Poultice made from bark and sap for wounds and cuts. Root decoction used for dysentery.
