Red Elderberry

Sambucus racemosa L. · Adoxaceae

Overview

Summary

Fast-growing Deciduous Shrub up to 2-6 m height with conical clusters of small white/creamy flowers, pithy hollow stems, and small shiny red berry clusters (leaves/stems/roots poisonous). Large shrub bearing clusters of red, waxy, pulpy berries.

Botanical Description

A fast-growing, large, deciduous shrub inhabiting moist to wet sites in nutrient-rich soils, reaching 2-6 m in height. Conical clusters of small white flowers turn to an array of small red berries in early summer that are a favourite of many local birds. It features thick, soft, pithy, usually somewhat glaucous twigs, dark reddish-brown warty bark, and conical clusters of small white to creamy flowers that develop into clusters of small, shiny, red, waxy, pulpy berries (leaves, stems, and roots are poisonous) which are favored by local birds. Foliage is opposite, stalked, large, and compound, pinnately divided into 5-7 elliptic to lanceolate leaflets that are often asymmetric at the base, abruptly sharp-pointed at the tip, saw-toothed on the margins, and usually somewhat hairy beneath.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Occurs in rich forests, woodlands, riparian areas, thickets, moist to mesic meadows, ditches, streambanks, grasslands, shrublands, disturbed areas, and forests in the lowland, steppe, and montane zones. Variety arborescens is common in and west of the Coast-Cascade Mountains, variety melanocarpa is common east of the Coast-Cascade Mountains and less frequent to the west, and variety leucocarpa is infrequent in south-central and southeastern British Columbia. Variety arborescens ranges north to Alaska and Yukon and south to California; variety melanocarpa ranges east to Alberta and south to New Mexico, Arizona, Nevada, and northern California; variety leucocarpa ranges east to Quebec and New Brunswick and south to Tennessee and Georgia. Also found in rich forests, woodlands, and partially shaded riparian wet areas.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Edible fruit, flowers. Food (edible berries processed into sauce), popguns made from hollow stems. Berries cooked into sauces (seeds strained/removed).

Edible Parts & Flavour

Cooked berries eaten in sauce/food (seeds removed due to cyanide causing nausea); hollow stems made into pea shooters; Berries edible (best cooked).

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Bark applied as poultice for boils and infections. Infusion of bark drunk to hasten childbirth during prolonged labour, and as purgative and emetic. Flowers/roots/bark made into medicinal tea.

Safety

Edibility

Yes

Pets, Children & Livestock

Generally non-toxic; harvest away from roadsides and pesticides, and confirm species ID before consumption.

Propagation & Seeds

Propagation Methods

Propagated by seed or cuttings. Fast-growing shrub. Red elderberry may be propagated vegetatively by dormant hardwood cuttings taken in late fall or winter, by softwood cuttings taken in the spring or summer, and by root or rhizome cuttings. Stem cuttings require at least 2 nodes (joints) with the basal cut just below the lower node.
