Pacific Sanicle

Sanicula crassicaulis Poepp.; (Suksd.) H. Wolff · Apiaceae

Overview

Summary

Monocarpic taprooted perennial 10-120 cm tall with thick stems; palmate 3-5 lobed leaves with sharp teeth; compact clusters of small yellow flowers; oval bur-like seeds. Frequent in southwestern BC (known from southern Vancouver Island, the Gulf Islands, and the adjacent coast) and infrequent in west-central BC (known from the Queen Charlotte Islands and Skeena River valley); ranges south to CA and MX, with a disjunct distribution in Chile, occupying open woods, meadows, shoreline bluffs, and forests on moist to dry sites from BC to Baja.

Botanical Description

A monocarpic taprooted perennial reaching 10-120 cm tall with thick stems, inhabiting local ecosystems from open meadows to dry forests where it forms a groundcover matrix and produces compact clusters of small yellow flowers that yield oval bur-like seeds. The leaves help form a groundcover matrix with other wildflowers and small yellow flowers turn to burr-like seeds. Plants are monocarpic (flowers once and then dies), usually flowering after several years. The plant features a solitary, erect stem measuring 25-120 cm tall and alternate leaves; the basal and lower stem leaves are palmately 3-5 lobed or divided without a narrow leaf axis, stalked below and becoming sessile above, with primary divisions merely lobed or toothed, and blades measuring 3-13 cm long and 2-18 cm wide.
