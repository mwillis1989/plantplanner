Broad-Leaved Stonecrop

Sedum spathulifolium Hook. · Crassulaceae

Overview

Summary

A low, hardy succulent perennial groundcover standing 5-20 cm tall with edible succulent leaves ranging from light/sage green to red, bearing showy bright yellow flowers. Inhabiting cliffs, coastal bluffs, forest openings, rocky outcrops, and rocky slopes, specifically occurring on mesic coastal cliffs and in forest openings within the lowland and montane zones, this species is common in southwestern British Columbia and known exclusively from Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands, extending south to California.

Botanical Description

A low, hardy succulent perennial groundcover standing 5-20 cm tall with edible succulent leaves ranging from light/sage green to red, bearing showy bright yellow flowers. It arises as a perennial herb from a stout rhizome and rootstock with several ascending to erect, glabrous and glaucous stems branched above, measuring 5-22 cm tall. Basal leaves are arranged in a rosette, and stem leaves are alternate, oblong to spoon-shaped or wedge-shaped, 0.5-2.5 cm long, succulent, flattened, broadest above the midlength and tapered to the base, strongly crowded, and glaucous, appearing pale green to reddish in full sun. Low succulent perennial (5–20 cm tall) forming rosettes of fleshy spoon-shaped leaves and bright yellow star-like flowers.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Inhabiting cliffs, coastal bluffs, forest openings, rocky outcrops, and rocky slopes, specifically occurring on mesic coastal cliffs and in forest openings within the lowland and montane zones, this species is common in southwestern British Columbia and known exclusively from Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands, extending south to California. It is well-suited for sunny rock gardens, containers, rock outcrops, concrete, cliffs, coastal bluffs, and forest openings.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Leaves/stems edible in moderation. Fleshy succulent leaves eaten raw as a tangy green. Edible succulent leaves.

Edible Parts & Flavour

Edible succulent leaves.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Medicinal use for burns and other injuries. Crushed leaves applied topically to soothe skin burns, insect bites, and minor wounds.
