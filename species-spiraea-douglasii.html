Hardhack

Spiraea douglasii Hook. · Rosaceae

Overview

Summary

Deciduous shrub up to 2 m in height with dense pinkish-purple flower spikes forming impenetrable thickets (alternatively characterized as a low to medium shrub, 0.5-2.5 m tall, from a creeping rhizome often forming thickets). Occurring in thickets, wetlands, riparian areas, fens, swamps, bogs, streambanks, lake margins, and moist to wet thickets and open forests in lowland and montane zones; common throughout British Columbia south of about 56° N, ranging south to California and Idaho, as well as sunny moist lake margins and wet meadows.

Botanical Description

Deciduous shrub up to 2 m in height with dense pinkish-purple flower spikes forming impenetrable thickets (alternatively characterized as a low to medium shrub, 0.5-2.5 m tall, from a creeping rhizome often forming thickets). Low to medium shrub, 0.5-2.5 m tall, from creeping rhizome, often forming thickets; stems erect, much-branched, the slender branchlets usually somewhat woolly, later becoming smooth. Leaves are alternate, deciduous, short-stalked, oblong to egg-shaped, 3-10 cm long, and coarsely toothed mostly above the middle, featuring a dark green and smooth upper surface and a paler, woolly-hairy to smooth lower surface. Thicket-forming shrub with pink/purple plume-like flower clusters and long, limber, curved stems.

Name Etymology

Genus Spiraea; specific epithet douglasii honors a botanical collector or naturalist associated with Hardhack.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Edible Parts & Flavour

Curved limber stalks used as fish spreaders during smoking/drying.

Material & Technology

Curved, limber stalks used as fish spreaders for drying or smoking fish to prevent curling. Fish-spreader material. Wood used to craft an implement for harvesting dentalium shells.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Medicine.

Propagation & Seeds

Propagation Methods

Rose spirea is readily propagated by softwood, semi - hardwood, and dormant hardwood cuttings, layering, rhizome and root segments (in spring), division, and seed.
