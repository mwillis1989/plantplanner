Overview

Summary

Perennial Forb, 50-100 cm height. Occurring in meadows and thickets, as well as moist to mesic salt marshes, meadows, slopes, and banks within the lowland zone.

Botanical Description

A perennial forb and herb growing from a rhizome or branching woody stem-base, possessing ascending to erect, hairy stems (at least above) measuring 0.5-1.0 m tall. Lowermost leaves are usually deciduous by flowering time, whereas stem leaves are typically oblanceolate and stalked below, transitioning to linear or lanceolate and smaller above, measuring 2-10 cm long and 2-20 mm wide, usually entire, and glabrous or sparsely hairy. Salt tolerant. Perennial Forb, 50-100 cm height.

Name Etymology

Genus Symphyotrichum; geographic specific epithet chilense denotes the plant's North American regional distribution.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Occurring in meadows and thickets, as well as moist to mesic salt marshes, meadows, slopes, and banks within the lowland zone. Common in coastal southwestern British Columbia and extending south to California (Meadows and thickets).

Propagation & Seeds

Stratification & Dormancy

Pacific aster does not possess seed dormancy, so untreated seed can be sown any time of year, but will generally germinate in the spring when soils are moist and temperatures reach 16°C. Standard site preparation should include treatment and/or removal of weeds for one to two years prior to seeding, and preparation of a clean, firm seed bed.

Propagation Methods

There are no developed cultivars of Pacific aster, but container plants and seed of local ecotypes are readily available from commercial sources throughout the west.
