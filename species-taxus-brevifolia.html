Pacific Yew

Taxus brevifolia 5; VIII b; Nutt. · Taxaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowTree; ShrubZone 4a–9b🦋 1 Lepidoptera hosts
🔎 Open 5-Tab Explorer Profile📊 Compare in Native Plant MatrixE-Flora BC ↗

Overview

Summary

Evergreen shrub or small tree (2-15 m) with fluted/twisted trunk, papery reddish scaly bark, flat fine-pointed needles in two rows, and single bony seed in bright red fleshy aril cup. Found on moist to mesic slopes and creek sides in the lowland and montane zones; common along the coast and in SC and SE BC; ranging north to SE AK and south to N CA, NE OR, ID, and NW MT; sporadic in moist mature/old-growth forest understory with Douglas-fir/Hemlock, along streams, wet slopes; above shoreline on Tl'ches.

Botanical Description

An evergreen shrub or small tree 2-15 m tall with a fluted or twisted trunk, papery reddish scaly bark, and branches spreading to drooping in flat sprays. Small tree 2-15 m tall, often shrubby; bark thin, with outer purplish scales over reddish to reddish-purple inner bark; male and female plants separate. Needles are 2-ranked, flattened, pointed at the tip, (12) 14-18 mm long, 1-2 mm broad, yellow-green and paler on the lower surface. Fruits develop as a round, red, fleshy aril 5-6 mm long borne on the underside of the branches; pollen cones are yellow, globe-shaped, and 3 mm long.

Successional Role

Evergreen, Shade-tolerant, long-lived. More shade tolerant than Pseudotsuga.

Fire Ecology

Appears in mature forest understory before severe fire. Succession to the Pseudotsuga "fire climax" succession.

Winter Interest

Moisture Notes

Prefers evenly moist, well-drained soils without standing winter water.

Root Formation

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota—typical of Cupressaceae & Taxaceae)

Competition / Allelopathy

Acidic needle duff, dense feeder-root competition, and year-round canopy shade out sun-loving meadow herbs; underplant with adapted acidophilic woodland understory flora.

Erosion & Slope Control

Suitable for shade-tolerant, moist forest slope restoration; persists above shorelines.

Fall & Winter Interest

Year-round evergreen foliage anchors winter garden structure.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Edible Parts & Flavour

Inedible / Toxic.

Material & Technology

Dense wood useful for tools, bows, paddles. Wood traditionally used by Coast Salish peoples to make specialized digging sticks for harvesting camas bulbs. Extremely hard wood carved for bows, wedges, clubs, paddles, digging sticks, adze handles, harpoon shafts, spears, mat-sewing needles, awls, dip-net frames, knives, dishes, spoons, boxes, combs, snowshoe frames, bark scrapers, fire tongs, weaving tools, drum frames, and seaweed processing blocks (Gitga'at).

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Aboriginal/Medicine/Material: Harvested for medicine and high-strength material/wood. Wood and bark used for medicinal teas to treat rheumatism, internal illnesses, heart problems, and cancer. Bark yields anti-cancer medicine (taxol).

Safety

Edibility

No / Toxic

Pets, Children & Livestock

Warnings, Toxicity & Pests

Seeds and foliage contain highly toxic taxine alkaloids fatal if ingested by humans, pets, or livestock. Poison garden.
