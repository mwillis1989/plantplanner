Western Red Cedar

Thuja plicata Donn. · Cupressaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowTreeZone 4a–9b🦋 68 Lepidoptera hosts

Overview

Summary

Massive evergreen coniferous tree (46–61 m / up to 60 m tall) with durable, clear-grained wood and fibrous reddish-brown bark; scale-like leaves with white markings underneath; small wooden seed cones. Forests, Riparian Areas, Woodlands.

Botanical Description

An evergreen coniferous tree of the cypress family (Cupressaceae) thriving in moist and nutrient-rich soils, reaching 50 (-75) meters tall (historically cited up to 46–61 m / 60 m), often buttressed at the base with a conical to irregular crown; older individuals frequently exhibit multiple leaders and dead spike tops. Branches are arching with pendent, flattened branchlets arranged in fan-shaped sprays, while the aromatic wood features durable, clear grain and a drooping growing tip (leader). Bark is gray to reddish-brown, 10-25 mm thick, ridged and fissured, readily tearing off in long, thin strips. Leaves are evergreen, scale-like, sharply pointed, (1-) 3-6 mm long, somewhat overlapping and closely pressed to the stem in opposite alternating pairs (4 rows), glossy green above with white stripes on the lower surface, turning brown and shedding with age, and emit a spicy fragrance when crushed.

Habitat & BC Range

Natural Habitat

Forests, Riparian Areas, Woodlands. Wet to moist floodplains, river terraces and slopes, often rich with nutrients from seepage or fluvial deposits, or rich parent material, in the lowland and montane zones; common along the coast and SC to SE BC, locally frequent in C BC. Canopy layer of Littlewood Forest, downslope/swamp area (MOḰEṈ); moist mixed conifer forests, swamps, lowlands, riparian areas. The range of western red-cedar is essentially in two segments: a Coast Range-Cascade Range segment from southeastern Alaska to northwestern California and a Rocky Mountain segment from British Columbia and Alberta to Idaho and Montana.

Habitat Types

Lowland to Montane (0–1200 m)

Successional Role

Evergreen, Low vitality, Moisture-loving successor. More shade tolerant than Pseudotsuga. Edaphic climax; early elimination by windthrow under extreme watertable fluctuations. In slopefoot situations with organic soils (humisols) Thuja plicata and Tsuga heterophylla will eventually dominate as shown in the AlAGP and AlAGt variants.

Garden Design & Growing

Garden Uses

Woodland / Shade; Rock Garden; Rain Garden / Wetland; Groundcover

Key Features

Pollinator Magnet; Butterfly / Moth Host; Deer Resistant; Winter Interest

Soil Preferences

The trees occur on various substrates, commonly on moist sites (swamps, wet ravines, poorly drained depressions), but on a variety of landforms, including rocky slopes, at 0-1500 (-2300) meters elevation. In cultivation, they prefer moist, acid, well-drained soils but have been grown in heavy clays of the Midwest.

Moisture Notes

Thrives in seasonally saturated soils, riparian benches, or poorly drained depressions.

Spread Behaviour

Woody crown and lateral root system

Root Formation

Deep lateral / Taproot / Woody burl

Mycorrhizal Association

Arbuscular Mycorrhizal (AM; Glomeromycota—typical of Cupressaceae & Taxaceae)

Competition / Allelopathy

Acidic needle duff, dense feeder-root competition, and year-round canopy shade out sun-loving meadow herbs; underplant with adapted acidophilic woodland understory flora.

Erosion & Slope Control

Recommended for replanting in Zones 3 & 5; key canopy tree in coastal forest restoration.

Pruning & Management

In mixed-species and uneven-aged stands, western red cedars tolerate shady understory conditions and can maintain slow but acceptable growth rates over long periods. In timber harvest of these mixed-species stands, most of these trees are taken by clearcutting. Because of steep terrain, decay, and breakage, harvesting costs are high and lumber recovery is low. Because of its high susceptibility to windthrow in wet environments and in the moist sites where growth and yield are highest, western red cedars should not be left as scattered seed trees.

Fall & Winter Interest

Distinctive winter bark and stem colour provide strong cold-season architectural interest.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Material & Technology

Bark fibers used for clothing, baskets, blankets, mats, cradle padding, sanitary pads, rope, and slow matches for fire. Wood used for houses and canoes (nuhw-lha lus, ‘uy’alus clear grain for planks). Inner bark (sléwaý) harvested and processed into strips to weave resilient wáts'iyus harvest baskets.

Medicinal (historical, not medical advice)

Various medicines were derived from the tree.

Safety

Propagation & Seeds

Stratification & Dormancy

Cone production begins in open-grown trees of western red cedar at about 10-20 years of age but peak production occurs after 70-80 years and may continue for several centuries. Good seed crops are produced at intervals of 2-3 years.
