Fool's Onion

Triteleia hyacinthina (Lindl.) Greene · Alliaceae

🌿 NativeBC CDC: YellowWildflower (Forb)Zone 4a–8b

Overview

Summary

Perennial Forb from corm, 30-60 cm height with umbel-shaped white flowers. Occurs in meadows, rocky slopes, and woodlands, specifically on dry to mesic grassy coastal bluffs, rocky slopes, and meadows in the lowland zone; frequent on southern Vancouver Island, the Gulf Islands, and the adjacent mainland; ranging south to Idaho, Nevada, and California.

Botanical Description

Perennial forb (30-60 cm) from corm with umbel-shaped white flowers. Leaves wither during flowering to form a unique tower of flower heads when planted in larger clusters; this drought-tolerant species is suited to both full sun and part shade. Plants arise from a deep, straw-coloured, fibrous-scaly, nearly globose, bulb-like corm (additionally propagating vegetatively from tan, fibrous-coated corms).

Pruning & Management

The area of white brodiaea will require regular weeding and the use of a very weak solution of fertilizer twice only during the active growth in the spring. The corms can be kept from overcrowding by thinning them every three years. This involves picking off the cormlets and replanting them elsewhere in the garden. There are five major types of indigenous management activities conducted in California that were designed to ensure future corm production at traditional gathering sites: (1) conscious breaking off cormlets from the harvested parent corms and replanting them; (2) sparing whole plants; (3) harvesting the corms after plants have gone to seed and dumping the seeds on the ground; (4) burning areas; and (5) irrigation.

Ethnobotany (Historical Records)

Food Uses

Bulbs eaten raw or cooked. Starchy corms dug, pit-cooked, or boiled as food alongside camas. Food (edible corms). Corms were dug with a digging stick and eaten either raw, roasted, baked in an earth oven, or boiled by tribes in California including the Southern Maidu, Northern Maidu, Washoe, Pomo, Sierra Miwok, Atsugewi, and Northern Paiute.

Safety

Edibility

Yes

Pets, Children & Livestock

Generally non-toxic; harvest away from roadsides and pesticides, and confirm species ID before consumption.

Propagation & Seeds

Stratification & Dormancy

The seed does not need to be stratified. Cold stratification is required.

Sowing Protocol

If possible, obtain the seed and corms from local sources to maintain genetic diversity of white brodiaea and for the best adaptation to local conditions. Some nurseries may label their corms and seeds according to geographic source. If planting flowering-size corms, they can be directly planted outside.

Propagation Methods

This plant is not readily available from commercial nurseries.
