Tower Mustard

Turritis glabra (L.) Bernh. · Brassicaceae

Overview

Summary

A biennial or short-lived perennial native mustard herb from a simple stem-base, growing 0.3-1.2 m tall (40-80 cm in height), with stems that are simple or branched above, densely stiff-hairy at the base, and usually smooth and hairless above. Inhabits rocky slopes, moist to dry streambanks, meadows, open forests, rock outcrops, roadsides, and waste places across lowland and montane zones; common throughout British Columbia; extending east to Quebec and south to Georgia and northern California; native status uncertain, also occurs in Eurasia.

Botanical Description

Biennial, Short-lived Perennial Forb, 40-80 cm height. Grows in sandy or rocky sites in full sun. A biennial or short-lived perennial native mustard herb from a simple stem-base, growing 0.3-1.2 m tall (40-80 cm in height), with stems that are simple or branched above, densely stiff-hairy at the base, and usually glabrous above. Basal leaves are oblanceolate and 3-14 cm long.
